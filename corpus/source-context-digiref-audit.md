# DigiRef / Reg. Ii source-critical context audit

Status: **evidence audit complete for the currently modelled Reg. Ii 3 / Reg. Ii 4 Bd. II / Reg. Ii 5 source groups and their immediately relevant 1527–1536 visitation context** (2026-09-06).

Evidence ledger: `corpus/source-context-digiref-evidence.tsv`.

This document is a source-critical context record, not a diplomatic transcription owner. It does not reopen completed Knau transcription/adjudication/collation work.

## 1. Scope and evidence rule

The audit inspected the DigiRef project documentation and index semantics, the official Archivportal Thüringen finding aid for the Ernestinisches Gesamtarchiv / Reg. Ii sequence, the project-relevant DigiRef/provider source-group records and saved DigiRef snapshots supplied for Reg. Ii 3, plus the already completed direct-image Reg. Ii 4/5 Knau collation.

It is **not** a mirror of all 9,652 portal objects. "Complete" here means complete for the presently relevant archival units, bounded source groups, chronology question and immediately surrounding visitation records needed to interpret them. Any future claim about another DigiRef object must be evidenced separately.

Evidence labels used below:

- **DIRECT-CATALOGUE** — official archive finding-aid statement;
- **DIRECT-DIGIREF** — DigiRef project documentation, record/index statement, or saved DigiRef detail page;
- **DIRECT-IMAGE** — conclusion from inspected manuscript images;
- **SECONDARY** — modern scholarly literature;
- **INFERENCE** — bounded conclusion from several direct observations;
- **OPEN** — not yet evidenced sufficiently for assertion.

A date attached to an archival unit is not silently transferred to each bounded source group. A DigiRef page hit is not silently treated as the documentary boundary of a source group. A modern filename is not dating evidence.

## 2. The hierarchy that must govern the corpus

The relevant structure is:

`archival unit / signature → bounded source group or document group → research locus → transcription units`

This is not merely terminological. It prevents three recurrent errors: treating a DigiRef title as the title of the entire archival file, treating a source-group folio range as the extent of the entire archival file, and transferring a date or person list from one descriptive level to another without evidence.

DigiRef itself distinguishes object types including **Akten** and **Visitationsprotokolle**. Its project documentation says that the research module selected 118 archivalia and uses "Protokoll" functionally for systematic capture of information about parish places. It also states that Beiakten, accounts and inventories were mostly not included in that research-module selection and that a place search normally lands on an **Einstiegspunkt**, often only the first page of a larger place block. Therefore a DigiRef search result is research navigation, not in itself an archival-boundary statement. See CTX-E001, CTX-E002 and CTX-E020.

## 3. Whole archival units: what is directly established

### Reg. Ii 3

**DIRECT-CATALOGUE:** official title *Visitation in Jena, Neustadt (Orla), Pößneck, Saalfeld (Saale)*; archival signature 3; dating **1529**; **525 leaves**.

The finding aid lists numerous internal groups. For the present research the most important are:

- Visitation in Neustadt an der Orla, fol. 41r–63v;
- Pfarreinkommen Ziegenrück (mit Eßbach), fol. 297v–302v;
- Pfarreinkommen Gössitz, Knau (mit Laskau, Bahren), Liebschütz (mit Liebengrün), fol. 303r–306r;
- Rezesse der Visitatoren 1529, fol. 508r–523r.

This proves that Reg. Ii 3 is much larger than any one of the project-facing source groups and that the Knau income material is embedded in a broad 1529 visitation/income/recess dossier. See CTX-E003.

### Reg. Ii 4 Bd. II

**DIRECT-CATALOGUE:** official title *Visitation in Thüringen*; archival signature **4/2**; dating **1533 [1534–1536]**; **418 leaves**.

It contains a long sequence of bounded groups across Weimar, Jena, Neustadt, Arnshaugk, Leuchtenburg and other districts. The group *Visitation im Amt Ziegenrück* is explicitly placed at **fol. 294r–313r**. It is therefore one component of the 418-leaf archival unit, not the whole unit. See CTX-E004.

The companion unit Reg. Ii 4 Bd. I is likewise titled *Visitation in Thüringen*, archival signature 4/1, dated **1533 [1534–1536]**, and contains 364 leaves. Together 4/1 and 4/2 form a much wider Thüringen visitation dossier than the Ziegenrück source group alone.

### Reg. Ii 5

**DIRECT-CATALOGUE:** official title *Registratur für die Superintendentur Neustadt (Orla) (Auszug)*; archival signature 5; dating **[1533–1536]**; **94 leaves**.

Its finding-aid contents include:

- Visitation in Neustadt (Orla), fol. 2r–3r;
- Visitation der Pfarren im Amt Arnshaugk, fol. 3v–27v;
- [Visitation im Amt Leuchtenburg und in Ziegenrück], fol. 28r–55r;
- Visitation im Amt Ziegenrück, fol. 55v–66v;
- [Visitation der Herrschaft Ranis], fol. 67r–74v;
- [Visitation im Amt Roda und in Jüdewein], fol. 75r–84v;
- later ordering/report material, fol. 86r–94r.

Thus Reg. Ii 5 is not "the Knau copy". It is a larger registratur/extract unit with a serially ordered selection of visitation material. See CTX-E005.

### Reg. Ii 6 and the wider sequence

Reg. Ii 6 is officially dated **1533–1535** and explicitly contains several *zweite Visitation* groups. In the surrounding sequence, Reg. Ii 1–3 are dominated by 1528/29 material, whereas Reg. Ii 4–7 contain substantial 1533–1536 material. This supports the larger chronological distinction between an earlier visitation complex and a later renewed/second visitation complex. See CTX-E006 and the official finding aid.

## 4. The earlier Neustadt / Ziegenrück context is not just 1529

A separate official file, archival signature **522**, is titled *Visitation in Weida, Neustadt (Orla), Saalfeld (Saale) und Pößneck* and dated **1527**. It includes a Neustadt group at fol. 23r–28r. This is direct evidence of visitation activity in Neustadt before Reg. Ii 3. See CTX-E007.

Accordingly, statements such as "the first visitation of Neustadt was 1529" are too broad. The safer chronology is:

- **1527:** earlier/provisional Neustadt-region visitation activity is directly documented;
- **1529:** Reg. Ii 3 preserves a large, systematic visitation/income/recess complex that includes Neustadt, Ziegenrück and Knau-related material;
- **1533 onward:** Reg. Ii 4/1–4/2 and the Reg. Ii 5 registratur belong to the later Thüringen visitation and administrative consolidation context.

The word "first" must therefore always be tied to the precise historical or project-defined sense in which it is being used.

## 5. What is established for the Reg. Ii 3 bounded groups

For `THHSTAW-EGA-REG-II-3-PROT-NEUSTADT-41R-63V`, the saved DigiRef detail page gives **23.5.–1.6.1529** as the visitation period. Its note says the visitors reached Neustadt on 23 May, began visiting surrounding communities on 24 May, and departed toward Pößneck on 1 June. The official whole-unit finding aid independently confirms the group at fol. 41r–63v. See CTX-E008.

For `THHSTAW-EGA-REG-II-3-STAT-VIS-00000235`, the saved DigiRef detail page gives the title and fol. 303r–306r but **no separate visitation period**. The official finding aid places it inside Reg. Ii 3, which is dated 1529. Therefore **1529 is established as archival-unit context**, while an exact date for this income source group remains unasserted. See CTX-E009.

DigiRef also indexes Ziegenrück within the Reg. Ii 3 Neustadt visitation and within the *Recesse der Visitatoren 1529*. These are independent signs that Ziegenrück belongs to the 1529 visitation transmission, but neither index hit converts the Knau income source group into an exactly dated 23 May–1 June protocol. See CTX-E010 and CTX-E018.

## 6. The later Reg. Ii 4 / Reg. Ii 5 context: what can be dated

The official Reg. Ii 4/2 finding aid dates the whole unit **1533 [1534–1536]** and places *Visitation im Amt Ziegenrück* at fol. 294r–313r. Reg. Ii 5 is dated **[1533–1536]** and places its Ziegenrück group at fol. 55v–66v. These are archival-unit dating statements, not exact local visit dates. See CTX-E004 and CTX-E005.

Separate 1533 administrative records provide strong year/cycle linkage to Ziegenrück:

- archival signature 590 concerns, among other matters, the Ziegenrück pastor and four acres of wood;
- archival signature 696 is correspondence between Johann Friedrich and the appointed Thüringen visitators concerning Johann Rost's vicarage at Ziegenrück;
- archival signature 604 asks whether the Neustadt visitation should be postponed until after the hay harvest once the Jena circuit was complete.

These records establish a **1533 Ziegenrück / Neustadt administrative visitation context** and show that the later visitation had an unfolding itinerary rather than one universal opening date. See CTX-E011–CTX-E013.

### Exact-date correction

The repository previously normalized the Reg. Ii 4 and Reg. Ii 5 Ziegenrück source groups as `1533-03-03` to `1533-12-31`. This audit located no direct source-group evidence that **3 March 1533** was the local Ziegenrück visitation date. A modern scholarly chronology assigns **03.03.1533 to Creuzburg**, while the official archival record for Neustadt explicitly discusses whether that circuit should be delayed until after hay harvest. See CTX-E011 and CTX-E017.

Consequently the exact normalized Ziegenrück interval has been removed from canonical `sources.tsv` and `events.tsv`. The evidence-supported claim is now deliberately narrower:

> **Ziegenrück belongs to the later Thüringen visitation cycle with direct 1533 administrative evidence; the exact local visit day represented by the Reg. Ii 4/5 Ziegenrück source groups remains open.**

This is an evidence downgrade in precision, not a move from 1533 to 1529.

## 7. Personnel: visitor group versus sovereign authority

Official descriptions of records 610 and 614 name the appointed Thüringen visitators as:

- Justus Menius;
- Friedrich Myconius;
- Georg von Wangenheim;
- Georg von Denstedt;
- Johann Kothe, mayor of Eisenach.

Johann Friedrich I appears in the surrounding administrative records as sovereign, issuer and correspondent. The event register now keeps him out of the flat visitator list rather than conflating commissioning authority with membership of the visitor commission. See CTX-E014.

The `persons` fields of individual bounded source groups remain source metadata and are **not** to be interpreted automatically as a role-resolved list of the visitators. A future person-role model may separate visitor, commissioner, local officer, patron and named subject if that becomes analytically necessary.

## 8. Reg. Ii 4 and Reg. Ii 5 are serially related, not merely a Knau pair

The official Reg. Ii 5 title already identifies the whole unit as an **Auszug**. Its internal sequence — Arnshaugk, Leuchtenburg/Ziegenrück, Ziegenrück, Ranis, Roda/Jüdewein — substantially parallels the order of corresponding groups in Reg. Ii 4/2. DigiRef place indexing also exposes parallels beyond Knau. See CTX-E004, CTX-E005 and CTX-E019.

For Knau itself, direct image collation is stronger than catalogue/index inference. Completed #99 established a selective/condensed textual correspondence between Reg. Ii 4 fol. 306v–309r and Reg. Ii 5 fol. 61v–63r: substantial shared sequence, but omissions, condensation and resegmentation. No line-for-line identity, no same-hand claim and no Reg. Ii 4 diplomatic correction followed. See CTX-E015.

Therefore the safest transmission statement is:

> **Reg. Ii 5 is an archival registratur/extract unit whose Ziegenrück material is catalogue-linked and directly textually related to Reg. Ii 4/2, but the exact mechanics, date and agency of the excerpting/redaction process remain to be demonstrated.**

## 9. Source-critical interpretation of the Knau material

The Knau passages should not be read as a transparent narrative report of a single moment. Across the archival context, the visitation process generated and assembled several kinds of record: local status/income information, inventory/account material, examination or reporting information, visitor decisions, recesses and later administrative implementation.

The Reg. Ii 4 Knau locus itself combines local ecclesiastical/economic description with normative settlement. Reg. Ii 5's selective preservation of only part of that complex is therefore historically meaningful: the excerpting stage is an act of administrative selection, not neutral duplication.

This distinction also changes how discrepancies are interpreted. An omission in Reg. Ii 5 can be evidence of selection or condensation; it is not automatically evidence that Reg. Ii 4 is corrupt. Conversely, a clearer Reg. Ii 5 phrase can suggest how a damaged Reg. Ii 4 passage once read, but it cannot be promoted into the diplomatic reading without direct Reg. Ii 4 support. That principle remains exactly the one applied in #99.

## 10. Current conclusions by evidence level

### Directly established

1. Reg. Ii 3, Reg. Ii 4/2 and Reg. Ii 5 are whole archival units substantially larger than the project-facing source groups.
2. Reg. Ii 3 is dated 1529 and contains separate Neustadt, Ziegenrück-income, Knau-income and recess groups.
3. Separate visitation activity in Neustadt is already documented in 1527.
4. Reg. Ii 4/2 is dated 1533 [1534–1536] and contains the Ziegenrück group at fol. 294r–313r.
5. Reg. Ii 5 is a 94-leaf registratur/extract unit dated [1533–1536] and contains the Ziegenrück group at fol. 55v–66v.
6. Ziegenrück is directly present in 1533 visitation administration outside the protocol groups themselves.
7. The appointed 1533 Thüringen visitators are Menius, Myconius, Wangenheim, Denstedt and Kothe in the cited official records.
8. Reg. Ii 4 and Reg. Ii 5 Knau loci are directly textually related but not line-identical.

### Bounded inference

The Reg. Ii 4/2 Ziegenrück group and the Reg. Ii 5 Ziegenrück extract belong to the **later/second Thüringen visitation and its 1533–1536 administrative-redaction context**, rather than to the Reg. Ii 3 1529 dossier. The year 1533 is strongly supported as event-cycle context; the precise local visit date is not.

### Open questions

- exact day or narrower interval of the Ziegenrück field visitation represented in Reg. Ii 4/5;
- exact production/redaction date of the Reg. Ii 5 extract;
- whether Reg. Ii 5 was excerpted directly in one operation from Reg. Ii 4/2 or through intermediate working material;
- hands, scribal roles and institutional responsibility for the copying/excerpting stages;
- role-resolved person modelling for the source-group metadata;
- whether additional surviving Beiakten permit reconstruction of the local itinerary and the movement from Jena/Neustadt through the Ziegenrück district more precisely.

## 11. Consequence for completed Knau work

Nothing in this audit invalidates #96–#99. Those stages established the diplomatic state and the directly observed Reg. Ii 4/5 textual relation. The present audit changes only the **external source context and confidence of dating metadata**:

`1527 earlier Neustadt visitation activity → 1529 Reg. Ii 3 visitation/income/recess complex → 1533ff. later Thüringen visitation and Reg. Ii 4/5 transmission context`

The next source-critical research step, if pursued, should target the **Beiakten and itinerary evidence for the 1533 Thüringen visitation around Jena, Neustadt, Leuchtenburg and Ziegenrück**, not reopen the Knau transcription.
