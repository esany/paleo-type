## Kommentar 5652157537 · 2026-09-13T08:15:16Z · esany

## Pilot 2 finding — Human feedback in Slack does not automatically reach a normal ChatGPT context

Observed during the live pilot on 2026-09-13:

The Human Owner posted a new top-level message directly in `#paleo-type-coordination` after `PILOT-171-B1`:

> Was passiert, wenn ich hier Feedback gebe? Wo wird das auch in chat gbt abgebildet/ bleibt nachvollziehbar/ referenziert.. Slack erzeugt ganz eigene konversationsstrulturen

That message remained in Slack until the active ChatGPT context was explicitly resumed and polled/read the channel. No normal ChatGPT context was automatically activated by the Human Owner's Slack feedback.

This is material pilot evidence, not a new METHOD or architecture decision.

### Implication

The passive-board pattern currently solves **content transport**, but not **feedback activation/routing**. Human feedback entered in Slack can become orphaned from the intended ChatGPT work context unless that context later polls the channel or an explicit event/wake mechanism exists.

Therefore Slack cannot currently serve as the primary Human-Owner control surface for normal chats. If Slack remains in the design, one of two boundaries must hold:

1. **Slack as bulletin/read surface only:** Human Owner steers work in ChatGPT; Slack shows deliveries/status/summary.
2. **Slack as accepted input surface:** a separate, explicitly admitted wake/dispatch mechanism must guarantee that Human-Owner feedback is delivered into the intended ChatGPT work context and made visible there.

Without (2), feedback typed only in Slack must not be assumed to have affected any chat, task or canonical state.

### Human-auditability concern

Slack also creates its own threads/top-level messages/replies. A Human Owner comment can therefore form a new conversation branch that is not automatically mirrored into the originating ChatGPT transcript. This weakens the desired single visible control history unless the chat later explicitly reads and summarizes that feedback.

No automation/bridge is authorized by this finding. It narrows the remaining pilot question: the unresolved problem is now **activation + human-feedback continuity**, not Slack read/write capability.

## Pilot 2 UX requirement — sender-side Slack permalink disclosure

Human Owner clarified an additional control/audit requirement during Pilot 2:

> whenever a ChatGPT work context posts to Slack, the sending chat should also carry the concrete link to that Slack post and keep the action transparent and controllable for the user.

Observed rationale:

- Slack creates its own conversation structure (top-level posts, threads, replies) outside the ChatGPT transcript;
- without a direct backlink, the Human Owner cannot reliably correlate what the chat says it sent with the actual Slack object;
- a visible permalink in the sending chat provides a direct inspection/control path without making Slack authoritative.

### Minimum pilot rule

For every consequential Slack send by a participating ChatGPT context, the local ChatGPT transcript should immediately expose:

1. the exact Slack permalink returned for that sent message;
2. a short human-readable statement of what was sent / why;
3. the relevant repo/task owner pointer when applicable;
4. whether the send is transport/review only or whether a durable consequence was separately persisted to the canonical repo owner.

The Slack permalink is an **audit/UX correlation pointer only**. It does not become Research-State-Authority and must not replace the canonical GitHub/repository owner.

This is currently a Pilot 2 control requirement / acceptance criterion, not yet a generic METHOD/schema decision.
