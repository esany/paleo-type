## Governing requirement
The structure must support historical claims only as far as inspected evidence and source criticism permit. Interpretation must remain separable from transcription, metadata and AI-generated plausibility.

## Review criteria
Verify that the research architecture can expose, where relevant:
- creation context, function/purpose and intended use;
- authorship/agency and institutional production context;
- record stage, copying/excerpting and transmission/dependence;
- genre, formularies and administrative practices;
- chronology, geography and institutional context;
- selection, survival, archival ordering and digitisation effects;
- corroboration and contradiction from genuinely independent evidence;
- source silence/absence without turning search absence into historical absence;
- explicit claim-to-evidence paths for consequential historical assertions;
- distinction between archive/catalogue claim, manuscript observation, identification and historical interpretation;
- limitations and alternative interpretations where evidence underdetermines the claim.

## Experiential-knowledge check
The system must allow experienced historical judgement about typical practices, source genres and plausibility to guide questions and comparison strategies while keeping that judgement visible as inference rather than evidence.

## Measures if a gap is demonstrated
Possible minimal measures:
- strengthen project-level interpretation/evidence notes rather than add generic schemas prematurely;
- improve source-audit records for literature/control evidence;
- make source-relationship and independence status more explicit;
- add claim/evidence links only when consequential historical arguments become hard to audit;
- use bounded corpus-discovery controls when completeness claims trigger #12;
- add independent specialist review for claims whose validity depends on deep historical expertise.

## Trigger
Review when the project begins producing substantive historical synthesis, when source relationships materially affect interpretation, before publication-level claims, or when reviewers cannot distinguish source text from inference/context.

## Definition of Done
- consequential historical claims can be traced to inspected evidence;
- source criticism and interpretive limits are visible;
- catalogue metadata and source content are not conflated;
- independent corroboration is distinguishable from repeated AI/context reuse;
- absence/completeness claims are bounded;
- a reviewer can challenge the reasoning without reconstructing hidden prompt context.

## Do not
Do not solve interpretive uncertainty by adding more prose confidence, entity links or model agreement. Do not turn a source database into an implicit historical argument.
