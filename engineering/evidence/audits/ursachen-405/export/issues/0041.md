## Text

## Priority
**P1 — foundational governance requirement.**

This issue should be resolved before any further material repository-wide restructuring, major methodology refactor, agent-architecture change, or systematic AI expansion. It is a prerequisite for the future structural audit in #25.

## Problem
The repository's governing objective is already expressed consistently across `README.md`, `AGENTS.md` and the scholarly method, but it is not yet established as a single highest-order canonical rule with explicit precedence and human-only change authority.

That creates a governance risk: a future AI/agent, refactor, software architecture change, standards migration or optimization could gradually reinterpret the repository's purpose while still appearing locally reasonable. Examples of unacceptable drift include:
- optimizing corpus scale, interoperability, automation or software quality at the expense of source-critical research quality;
- treating a current technical architecture, data format or standard as a goal in itself;
- reducing independent expert review because model agreement appears sufficient;
- shifting specialist adjudication onto the non-specialist project owner;
- conflating human usability with a UI while weakening epistemic transparency;
- replacing visible uncertainty with completion, normalization or synthetic confidence;
- allowing project convenience to redefine archival, palaeographic, editorial or historical requirements.

Git history makes such drift recoverable after the fact, but the repository currently lacks a clear constitutional rule that makes it invalid before/at review time.

## Governing objective to protect
The repository exists to provide **scholarly excellent, evidence-constrained and practically usable support for the critical study and unlocking of early-modern historical sources, especially handwritten manuscript material, as a transdisciplinary research problem**.

All other requirements are subordinate and derived from this objective.

The repository must therefore support, as applicable:
- historical source criticism;
- archival science / diplomatics;
- palaeography and scribal evidence;
- scholarly editing / textual criticism;
- historical philology / language history / lexicography;
- codicology / material manuscript study;
- onomastics / toponymy;
- historical metrology / numismatics / accounting notation;
- source-type/domain history;
- research-data stewardship / Good Research Practice;
- research software / data engineering;
- AI engineering with genuine Human-in-the-loop and independent specialist validation where consequential.

Formal standards, disciplinary guidance, peer-reviewed methodological work, high-quality scholarly practice and experiential/tacit domain knowledge must be used according to their evidential role and re-checked against the then-current state of the art when material decisions are made.

## Required precedence hierarchy
The repository must make the following hierarchy explicit and normative:

`GOVERNING OBJECTIVE`
`→ scholarly/research requirements`
`→ disciplinary/epistemic requirements`
`→ methodology and research gates`
`→ corpus/project information model`
`→ Human-in-the-loop / validation workflow`
`→ software, AI tooling, automation, GitHub workflow and presentation`

Lower layers may implement, refine or operationalize higher layers. They may **not redefine or weaken them for convenience**.

When two rules conflict, the rule closer to the governing scholarly objective wins unless the human project owner explicitly changes the objective itself.

## Required canonical artifact
Create one small top-level canonical file, preferably:

`GOVERNING_OBJECTIVE.md`

The file must be short, stable and implementation-independent. It is a constitution/mission boundary, **not another methodology handbook**.

It should contain only the highest-order invariants needed to prevent mission drift.

### Minimum contents

#### 1. Primary scholarly purpose
- support reliable critical access to and understanding of early-modern historical sources, especially manuscripts;
- scientific/scholarly result and source understanding are the product;
- tooling, AI, data structures and repository mechanics are support infrastructure only.

#### 2. Transdisciplinary requirement
- the source/problem determines which scholarly domains must be activated;
- no single discipline or software model is sufficient by default;
- the framework must remain able to route to domain-specific methods and expertise without hard-coding the current pilot source.

#### 3. Evidence and epistemic integrity
- evidence precedes plausibility;
- source observation, reading, normalization, identification and interpretation remain distinct;
- uncertainty is a legitimate result;
- metadata/model/context cannot silently become source evidence;
- AI output is not evidence by itself;
- stronger claims require proportionally stronger evidence/validation.

#### 4. State-of-the-art and experiential knowledge
- relevant standards/guidance/practice must be current when consequential decisions depend on them;
- today's TEI/RiC/GWP/AI practices are not frozen forever;
- experiential/tacit specialist knowledge is required where relevant, but must generate inspectable hypotheses, comparisons and tests rather than operate as invisible authority;
- contested disciplinary choices remain explicit choices rather than being flattened into generic 'best practice'.

#### 5. Human-in-the-loop and accountability
- AI supplies proactive domain expertise, candidates, analysis and state maintenance as evidence permits;
- the non-specialist project owner controls purpose, priorities, access/rights facts and consequential use/publication decisions;
- the user is not required to act as substitute palaeographer/historian/archivist for specialist adjudications they cannot independently validate;
- independent qualified specialists are an escalation/validation layer for consequential claims where procedural/AI evidence is insufficient;
- AI-assisted method compliance and independent expert validation remain distinct statuses.

#### 6. Human auditability
- a non-specialist owner must be able to understand the active source/result, uncertainty, evidence basis, validation state and next meaningful research action without reconstructing agent prompts or machine-oriented tables;
- a specialist must be able to drill down from a claim to exact source evidence and decision history;
- human-readable views must not become a second manually maintained truth store.

#### 7. Technical subsidiarity / lean principle
- choose the least complex structure that preserves required scholarly distinctions, traceability, human usability and reproducibility;
- software/data/AI architecture is justified only by a demonstrated scholarly requirement, error class, workload or interoperability need;
- complexity is not quality;
- technologies and standards implementations remain replaceable means.

#### 8. Canonical-state / reproducibility principle
- durable consequential state must be recoverable from repository + accessible sources without old chat;
- one fact/decision/state has one canonical owner;
- derived views, indexes and interfaces must remain distinguishable from canonical research truth.

#### 9. Precedence rule
State explicitly:

> If methodology, data model, AI optimization, automation, GitHub workflow, project convention or implementation convenience conflicts with the governing scholarly objective, the governing objective prevails.

#### 10. Change authority
State explicitly:

> The governing objective may be analysed and challenged by AI or collaborators, but it may not be materially redefined, weakened or superseded without an explicit decision by the human repository/project owner.

AI may propose an amendment. AI must not infer owner consent from prior prompts, implementation convenience, a standards update, repeated model agreement or an architecture review.

## Required repository integration
After creating the canonical objective:

1. `README.md`
   - identify `GOVERNING_OBJECTIVE.md` as the highest-level purpose/constraint;
   - keep README as orientation, not a duplicate constitution.

2. `AGENTS.md`
   - add an explicit first-order rule that all AI-assisted work is subordinate to `GOVERNING_OBJECTIVE.md`;
   - add a hard stop against autonomously changing/weakening the governing objective;
   - require a mission-alignment check before consequential METHOD/CORPUS/architecture changes.

3. `methodology/historical-source-workflow.md`
   - state that the scholarly method operationalizes the governing objective and cannot supersede it.

4. `methodology/maintenance.md`
   - treat a proposed governing-objective change as a special governance event, not ordinary maintenance;
   - no automatic propagation/refactor may silently alter the objective.

5. `DECISIONS.md`
   - record the durable cross-project decision that the governing objective has precedence over derived architecture/method and requires explicit human authority to change.

6. `#25`
   - make this issue a prerequisite/reference for the transdisciplinary structural audit;
   - any later structural audit must demonstrate alignment with the governing objective before implementation.

Do not duplicate the full objective text into all of these files. Reference the canonical file and keep local consequences minimal.

## Required AI anti-drift rules
The implementation must explicitly protect against these failure modes:

### Goal substitution
An AI may not replace the scholarly objective with a measurable proxy such as throughput, transcription completion, automation rate, corpus size, number of resolved tokens, standards compliance, schema completeness or software quality.

### Means-end inversion
A technology, data model, standard, workflow or agent system may not become a primary success criterion merely because effort has already been invested in it.

### Local optimization
A change that improves one subdomain but weakens another required scholarly domain must not be accepted without explicit cross-domain analysis.

### Silent scope narrowing
The framework may not become overfit to Knau/visitation records such that it no longer supports comparable early-modern manuscript/source work.

### Silent epistemic weakening
Faster/easier workflows may not collapse uncertainty, provenance, witness independence, source-phenomenon distinctions, review status or claim/evidence separation.

### Human-role drift
Human-in-the-loop may not degrade into repeated user confirmation of specialist judgements the user cannot assess.

### AI self-legitimation
Model agreement, self-review or adherence to its own prompt rules may not be treated as independent validation.

### Standard fetishism
Compliance with TEI, RiC, FAIR, GWP, an AI benchmark or any other framework is not sufficient evidence of scholarly adequacy by itself.

## Required change-control policy
A material change to `GOVERNING_OBJECTIVE.md` must:
- occur in a dedicated/cohesive PR, never as incidental cleanup;
- state exactly what invariant changes and why;
- identify affected scholarly domains;
- distinguish clarification from material mission change;
- explain consequences for methodology, projects, AI/HITL and software architecture;
- review then-current standards/expert practice where relevant;
- preserve prior rationale in Git history / `DECISIONS.md`;
- receive explicit approval from the human repository/project owner before merge.

Routine changes elsewhere may remain lean and need not inherit this ceremony.

## Technical protection to evaluate/implement
The repository currently has no ruleset and `main` is unprotected. This issue must therefore evaluate and implement the smallest GitHub-native protection that makes accidental/autonomous mission changes materially harder without blocking normal research work.

Preferred target behavior:
- ordinary project/transcription/research-state changes can remain efficient;
- `GOVERNING_OBJECTIVE.md` and possibly closely related governance files cannot be silently altered by an autonomous AI workflow;
- material mission changes require a visible PR and explicit human approval.

Candidate mechanisms to evaluate:
- branch protection / repository ruleset requiring PR review for protected changes;
- `CODEOWNERS` plus required code-owner review if it can provide the needed path-level human approval;
- equivalent minimal GitHub-native mechanism available for this repository/account.

Do not overprotect the entire repository if a narrower mechanism satisfies the requirement. Do not introduce external governance infrastructure unless GitHub-native controls are insufficient.

## Verification / tests
The implementation is not complete merely because the new file exists. Test at least these scenarios conceptually and, where GitHub controls allow, operationally:

1. **Automation proposal** — an agent proposes replacing manual scholarly inspection with scaled AI because throughput improves. The objective must require evidence/validation and reject throughput as the governing criterion.
2. **Standards migration** — a new TEI/RiC version appears. The system may update derived method/representation but must not redefine scholarly purpose.
3. **Model improvement** — a future model performs much better on manuscripts. The system may change workflow after evaluation but may not replace independent validation where consequence still requires it.
4. **New source type** — a different early-modern manuscript enters the corpus. The objective must force domain/risk re-evaluation rather than Knau-specific assumptions.
5. **User usability conflict** — a technically elegant representation becomes unreadable to the non-specialist owner. Human auditability remains a requirement.
6. **Direct AI edit attempt** — an AI/refactor attempts to weaken or remove an invariant as part of cleanup. Governance/change control should block or visibly surface the change for human approval.
7. **Legitimate human amendment** — the owner explicitly chooses to change the mission. The process must allow this through a visible, documented decision rather than pretending the objective is eternally immutable.
