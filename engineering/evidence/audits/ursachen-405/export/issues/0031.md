## Governing requirement
The repository must be able to activate the right historical domain expertise for the concrete source and question—ecclesiastical, administrative, legal, economic/social or other—without hard-coding the Knau pilot's subject matter into the generic framework.

## Review criteria
Verify that a future structure supports:
- explicit routing from research question/source phenomenon to the relevant domain expertise;
- source-type-specific concepts, institutions, practices and formularies without treating them as transcription evidence;
- clear provenance for domain claims from inspected primary sources, editions and scholarship;
- separation of generic method from project/source-type knowledge;
- reusable domain knowledge only when it genuinely recurs across projects;
- project-local hypotheses/leads remaining local until independently supported and demonstrably generalizable;
- historical terminology/concepts remaining distinct from manuscript forms;
- multiple plausible institutional/legal/economic interpretations when evidence does not discriminate;
- escalation to qualified domain experts for consequential claims outside reliable procedural validation.

## Experiential-knowledge check
The system should benefit from tacit expert knowledge—what records normally contain, institutional practices, legal/economic conventions, likely source functions—while forcing that experience to produce explicit questions, candidate explanations and evidence tests rather than invisible conclusions.

## Measures if a gap is demonstrated
Possible minimal measures:
- improve expertise-routing rules rather than create a domain ontology;
- create project/domain notes with inspected-source provenance when repeated concepts matter;
- promote genuinely reusable concepts only after cross-project recurrence;
- add targeted research-source audits or specialist review packets;
- introduce structured domain vocabularies only when inconsistent terminology or interoperability is a demonstrated problem.

## Trigger
Review when a new project/source type enters the repository, when domain assumptions repeatedly contaminate readings/interpretations, when the same specialist concepts recur across projects, or when consequential historical conclusions depend on specialist institutional knowledge.

## Definition of Done
- the governing domain for consequential specialist claims is identifiable;
- domain knowledge is traceable and separable from manuscript evidence;
- the generic framework remains source-type agnostic;
- project-specific expertise does not leak into METHOD without a generalisability test;
- independent expert review is visible where domain-specific stakes warrant it.

## Do not
Do not build a universal early-modern knowledge base in advance. Do not encode the Knau visitation context as the default model for unrelated manuscripts.

# Der AI Engineer hätte noch einen anderen Kritikpunkt

Er würde sehr aufmerksam auf dieses Muster schauen:

> Die KI schreibt Regeln darüber, wie die KI kontrolliert werden soll.

Das ist nicht grundsätzlich falsch. Aber es birgt eine klassische Schwäche:

**self-referential governance.**

Wenn dasselbe Modell:

1. liest,
2. entscheidet,
3. dokumentiert,
4. den Integrity Check ausführt,
5. feststellt, dass alles okay ist,

dann ist das kein unabhängiger Kontrollmechanismus.

Das Repo weiß das fachlich bereits:

> Modellwiederholung ist keine unabhängige Evidenz.

und

> method-compliant AI work ≠ independent expert validation.

Ein AI Engineer würde sagen: Sehr gut. Jetzt muss diese Einsicht später auch **technisch in unterschiedliche Kontrollformen** übersetzt werden.

Beispiele wären irgendwann:

* deterministic schema checks,
* source/data consistency scripts,
* unabhängiger zweiter Modelllauf mit bewusst anderem Kontext,
* adversarial review prompts,
* gezielte Human Review,
* eventuell andere Modelle für bestimmte Fehlerklassen.

Nicht jetzt alles bauen.

Aber **AI Governance sollte langfristig nicht ausschließlich Prompt Governance bleiben.**
