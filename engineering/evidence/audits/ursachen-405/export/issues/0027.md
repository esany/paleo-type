## Governing requirement
The structure must help experts and AI test difficult manuscript readings from visible scribal evidence without turning lexical or historical plausibility into graphemes.

## Review criteria
Verify that the representation supports:
- localisation of uncertain sign/grapheme sequences before word completion;
- exact, recoverable comparison loci;
- distinction between same record, same document context and actually supported same-hand evidence;
- explicit hand-identification uncertainty and separation of hand from named scribe/person;
- grapheme-level discriminating features rather than silhouette matching;
- abbreviations, numerals, monetary/metrological/accounting notation as potentially distinct sign systems;
- secure anchors, candidate readings, alternatives, rejected readings and residual uncertainty;
- source-image provenance for consequential visual comparison;
- separation of palaeographic evidence from lexical, contextual and historical inference;
- escalation to qualified palaeographic review for consequential unresolved specialist readings.

## Experiential-knowledge check
A future design must make room for expert pattern knowledge—script habits, ductus, common abbreviation systems, numeral forms, scribal variation—while requiring that such experience generates comparisons/tests rather than functioning as invisible authority.

## Measures if a gap is demonstrated
Possible minimal measures:
- strengthen locus-addressed comparison records;
- separate hand hypothesis/status from source identity;
- refine palaeographic evidence/concordance structures based on real recurring needs;
- create a corpus-level hand registry only when the trigger in #22 is genuinely met;
- add image/crop provenance for comparison anchors if current traceability is insufficient;
- introduce dedicated abbreviation/numeral comparison structures only for observed recurring systems;
- define an independent expert-review packet/view for consequential cases.

## Trigger
Review when difficult readings repeatedly fail because evidence cannot be compared/recovered, when hand attribution becomes consequential across records, when palaeographic evidence no longer fits project-local tables, or before publication claims that depend materially on specialist readings.

## Definition of Done
- every consequential palaeographic conclusion can be traced to exact manuscript loci and comparison evidence;
- same-hand claims are evidenced, not inferred from context;
- competing graphical readings remain visible where evidence does not discriminate;
- specialist experience is inspectable through its evidential tests;
- unresolved evidence is allowed;
- independent human palaeographic review status is explicit where stakes require it.

## Do not
Do not build a universal grapheme ontology, hand-recognition system or HTR pipeline merely to appear sophisticated. HTR remains governed by #3 and any hand registry by #22.

### Real-data falsification / Knau

The current Knau material provides representative failure/test cases without defining the generic model:

- `132.tsv`: `[W1]`–`[W6]`, `[U1]`/`[U2]`, repeated surname token, `It(em)` and `Same-hand recheck required` demonstrate the need to separate visible span, candidate, task, expansion and hand-evidence state.
- `133.tsv`: two observed `80` forms are useful positive anchors, while the unresolved upper passage shows that uncertainty can be preserved without semantic completion.
- `134.tsv`: paragraph-level placeholders are deliberately conservative but are not yet adequate physical/grapheme localization for G2/G3.
- `135.tsv`: fully reinspected lines provide useful secure anchors/control examples.
- `palaeography/numerals.tsv`: good existing safeguard — C2 accounting notation is explicitly comparison-class evidence only because hand identity with Reg. Ii 5 is not established.
- `palaeography/lexicon.tsv`: useful working inventory, but repeated word forms alone are not yet a palaeographic evidence record because discriminating graphical features and exact anchors are absent.

### Architecture handoff — not implementation

Material findings for #36/#25:

1. **Exact palaeographic anchor addressing** is required; coordinate with the locus-addressing finding from #26 rather than inventing a separate addressing scheme.
2. A minimal representation for **hand hypotheses/status/evidence** may be needed if real work requires hand-based comparanda; do not create a corpus hand registry until #22's trigger is met.
3. Consequential internal comparison needs a lean way to preserve **candidate → graphical evidence/features → anchors → conclusion/residual uncertainty**. This may be project-local; no universal grapheme ontology is justified.
4. Abbreviation/expansion and specialist notation must be representable without collapsing source sign and editorial interpretation; test this in the later Knau loss test.
5. Exact image/crop provenance should be attached only when a visual derivative materially participates in a consequential comparison.

### Overall classification

- Generic palaeographic method/gates: **adequate**.
- Legacy Knau G2/G3 representation: **gap/partial**, as already acknowledged by project status.
- Hand identity modelling: **gap but trigger-sensitive**; no evidence for a global registry now.
- Internal numeral/notation evidence discipline: **strong partial implementation**.
- Need for HTR, hand-recognition, grapheme ontology or complex palaeography database: **not demonstrated**.
