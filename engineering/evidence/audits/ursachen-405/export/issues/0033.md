## Governing requirement
Person/place identification must help historical interpretation and discovery without silently normalizing or rewriting manuscript name forms.

## Review criteria
Verify that the structure supports:
- manuscript name form separately from normalized identity;
- multiple candidate identities and explicit uncertainty;
- variant historical spellings without forced canonicalization;
- evidence for identification from inspected sources, historical context and verified authority data;
- distinction between palaeographic reading, linguistic normalization and historical identification;
- temporal/geographical disambiguation;
- conflicting identifications across projects/sources;
- stable local entity identity when repeated use justifies it;
- optional external authority identifiers only when verified;
- traceability of why an identification was accepted, rejected or left unresolved.

## Experiential-knowledge check
Expert familiarity with regional name forms, historical settlements, family names and spelling variation may generate candidates and search strategies, but must not itself turn an unresolved manuscript token into a secure identification.

## Measures if a gap is demonstrated
Possible minimal measures:
- improve entity-evidence/status fields or project-level identification notes;
- preserve explicit candidate sets and rejected identifications;
- add spatial/temporal disambiguation where real collisions occur;
- perform external authority reconciliation only when the trigger in #6 is reached;
- introduce reusable place/person registries only for recurring cross-project identities;
- use specialist onomastic/toponymic review for consequential ambiguous identifications.

## Trigger
Review when duplicate/conflicting person/place identities recur, when normalized entities begin driving corpus search/analysis, when external authority interoperability becomes useful, or when a historical argument depends materially on an uncertain identification.

## Definition of Done
- manuscript forms remain untouched by normalization;
- entity claims expose evidence and uncertainty;
- duplicate/conflicting candidates can coexist until resolved;
- external authority links do not become evidence by themselves;
- independent specialist review status is visible where identification is consequential.

## Do not
Do not auto-merge entities by spelling similarity. Do not let modern place/person names become diplomatic readings. Do not make authority-control infrastructure mandatory before actual ambiguity requires it.

## Domain audit result — onomastics and toponymy

Scope: requirements audit only. No entity merge, authority reconciliation or manuscript normalization is performed.

Reference basis: #38; current entity registries, Knau transcription/context notes and source relations.

### Concrete repo evidence

- `entities/places.tsv` keeps `PLACE-KNAU` (`Knau`) separate from manuscript form `Knawe` and records a working evidence basis.
- `entities/persons.tsv` stores `PERSON-WOLF-GRAEFENDORF` only as `working` and explicitly says the normalized identity must not overwrite the unresolved manuscript surname token.
- `context-notes.md` preserves the Gräfendorf identification as a candidate and requires source-based verification.
- External authority IDs are currently not invented.

### Architecture handoff

No authority-control platform or universal entity graph is justified. Later architecture should support a small, explicit identification-claim path where real ambiguity exists: `(manuscript locus/form → candidate entity/entities → evidence → status/decision/review)`. Corpus-level entity rows remain reusable normalized identities, not manuscript-reading records.

Overall: current separation is **strong**; candidate/conflict/evidence traceability is the main future gap when identifications become consequential.
