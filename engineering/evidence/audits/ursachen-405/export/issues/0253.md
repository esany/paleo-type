## Problem / Gap
F10 is a demonstrated but orthogonal failure: a fresh reviewer can inherit the same contested premise from derived/current research owners, so `fresh context` is not equivalent to an independent evidential basis. Better engineering traceability can expose provenance but does not itself prove epistemic independence.

## Required effect
Determine the smallest adequate safeguard that lets consequential review distinguish:
- directly reinspected/independent evidence;
- inherited premise/derived owner;
- same-evidence re-judgement;
- genuinely independent support;
without turning all review into duplicated scholarship or a global provenance ontology.

The real #171 failure was reconstructed: a fresh review context can inherit the same derived premise stack. Competing responses were tested. The selected proportional safeguard is **review-evidence-basis + contested-premise descent**: state the premise, what evidence/owner was actually inspected, what remained inherited, and do not call a new context/model independent evidence merely because it is new.

Disposition: existing epistemic meaning is sufficient; future review/context surfaces need this operational evidence-basis field/behavior. No separate EKCP subsystem, graph, Bridge, workflow or global provenance ontology is warranted. #253 is complete at the present evidence state.
