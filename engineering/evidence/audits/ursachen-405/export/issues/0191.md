## Motivation

#186 found stronger positive evidence for bounded consequence/adequacy judgement and controlled scale/context switching than for any generic research-question-generation mechanism. It explicitly records that no fixture has yet demonstrated and compared several serious next research questions from the same rich state, and that the A1/A5 gap therefore remains partly an **absence-of-demonstration** problem.

That gap can materially change the later Capability/Requirement decision:

- if heterogeneous fixtures produce different substantive questions but recurring scholarly operations, a thin generic enabling need may be evidenced;
- if question formation remains largely source-/purpose-/domain-specific, later synthesis should not manufacture a large generic `Research Framing` capability;
- if no responsible plurality of serious questions can be generated from the available states, that negative result is itself Research evidence.

## Primary experiment

For each fixture, without Candidate-Question schema or quantitative/scoring model, ask:

> Which several serious research questions arise from this current knowledge state, why are they scientifically interesting, what evidence could discriminate or materially advance them, and what evidential/source-purpose boundaries constrain them?

Do not auto-authorize any generated question as Project/task scope.

Record enough dependency provenance to reconstruct which source feature, contextual information or fachliche perspective generated or materially changed each question.

## Targeted disciplinary challenge

After questions are generated, challenge them against source-type-relevant specialist/editorial scholarship and direct source evidence.

Use a deliberately small challenge surface, primarily historical method/source criticism plus source-type expertise where materially needed. This is **literature-grounded disciplinary challenge, not independent human specialist validation**, unless such human evidence is separately obtained.

Reject or weaken questions that:

- merely restate an unresolved detail without scientific consequence;
- require evidence not available or not identifiable;
- silently treat a source address as the research object;
- infer lived practice from a normative/administrative record without source-critical support;
- import later/contextual knowledge as if it were direct source evidence;
- depend on a source-purpose assumption not carried by the fixture;
- collapse editorial, historical and technical questions without justification.

## Comparison question

Only after fixture-level challenge ask:

> Across the three cases, do any recurring scholarly operations in question formation appear, even though the substantive questions differ?

Possible recurrence must arise from the observations. Do not force prior Concept-Candidate terms.
