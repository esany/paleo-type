## Governing requirement
The structure must support historically informed reading and normalisation without allowing linguistic plausibility to overwrite visible manuscript evidence.

## Review criteria
Verify that the system can keep distinct and traceable:
- graphematic/diplomatic manuscript form;
- segmentation and word-boundary uncertainty;
- historical spelling and morphological variation;
- abbreviation sign versus editor-provided expansion;
- lexical candidate/identification versus manuscript reading;
- normalization for search/use versus diplomatic source text;
- historical semantic change and period-appropriate usage;
- lexical/philological evidence from inspected dictionaries, editions or scholarship;
- uncertainty when a form is palaeographically possible but lexically unclear, or vice versa;
- language evidence that helps discriminate candidates only after visible evidence is preserved.

Review whether recurring lexical/linguistic evidence belongs in project evidence, a corpus-level reusable lexicon, or external research-source audit rather than being embedded invisibly in prompts.

## Experiential-knowledge check
The design should enable expert knowledge of historical orthography, morphology, abbreviations, formulaic language and semantic change to generate plausible tests while making clear what comes from source observation, comparative language evidence and editorial inference.

## Measures if a gap is demonstrated
Possible minimal measures:
- separate expansion/normalization/lexical-identification fields or records where currently collapsed;
- improve provenance for lexicographic evidence;
- create a reusable lexicon only when repeated cross-project use justifies corpus-level ownership;
- add explicit segmentation/word-boundary uncertainty where observed;
- structure bibliography only if the maintenance trigger in #13 is met;
- add targeted expert review for consequential philological identifications.

## Trigger
Review when normalisation/expansion repeatedly contaminates diplomatic readings, when historical word forms cannot be traced to evidence, when lexical work is duplicated across projects, or when publication-level claims depend on specialist linguistic identification.

## Definition of Done
- manuscript form, expansion, normalization and lexical identification remain separable;
- linguistic evidence is source-traceable and does not act as hidden authority;
- ambiguous historical forms can remain unresolved;
- cross-project language knowledge is stored only when genuinely reusable;
- independent specialist review status is explicit for consequential philological claims.

## Do not
Do not modernise the transcription to simplify NLP/search. Do not build a universal historical-language ontology before repeated source evidence requires one.

## #28 domain audit — historical philology / language history / lexicography

Scope: requirements audit only. No transcription, schema, methodology or architecture change is authorized here.

### Current reference frame
- **FWB-online / Frühneuhochdeutsches Wörterbuch** is directly period-relevant: it describes High German vocabulary from the mid-14th to mid-17th century and preserves historical word variants, morphology, semantics, syntagms and source attestations. It is a lexicographic evidence source, not a palaeographic authority.
- **Deutsches Rechtswörterbuch (DRW)** is a major historical lexicographic source where legal/administrative vocabulary is relevant; use remains claim- and context-specific.
- Historical corpora (e.g. CLARIN/BBAW historical corpora where temporally/source-typologically relevant) can test attested forms/usages, but corpus frequency does not create manuscript graphemes.
- TEI remains representation guidance only; no encoding standard determines a philological reading.

### Real Knau loss/risk test
Representative cases demonstrate why the distinctions matter:
- `grevendorff / greuendorff / grefendorff`: a lexical/onomastic identification cannot resolve the graphematic spelling; current handling correctly preserves alternatives.
- `It(em)`: the manuscript abbreviation and the editorial expansion are conceptually distinct, but the legacy flat reading makes the boundary less explicit than the generic guideline requires.
- `[W1]–[W6]`: historical-economic vocabulary/notation may eventually become lexically interpretable, but current unresolved sign groups must remain primary until palaeographic/notational evidence supports a reading.
- withdrawn fluent reconstructions on fols. 62r–62v show a real observed failure class: syntax/semantic plausibility can over-smooth manuscript evidence.

### Main audit conclusion
The repo does **not** need a historical-language ontology, NLP normalization pipeline or corpus-wide lexicon at this point. The actual demonstrated gaps are narrower:
1. explicit representation of segmentation/word-boundary uncertainty;
2. a cleaner source-vs-expansion distinction in the eventual G2 representation;
3. inspectable provenance for lexical/semantic evidence when FWB/DRW/corpora or scholarship are actually used;
4. a separate status/evidence path for later normalization/lexical/semantic claims rather than reusing transcription status.

These findings are handed to the later Knau loss test and #36/#25. They do not authorize implementation now.

No manuscript reading, research gate, source relation or project status was changed by this audit.
