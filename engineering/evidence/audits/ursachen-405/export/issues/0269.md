## Status / authority

**BUG / ANALYSIS RECORD ONLY. No relationship ontology or contract change is authorized by this Issue.**

## Demonstrated gap

The repository's scholarly rule is already clear:

- D-005 / REQ-ARCH-006 require Source relationships such as `copy of`, `extract from`, `parallel to` or `textually corresponds to` to be explicit evidence-bearing claims rather than identity shortcuts;
- catalogue/finding-aid metadata can support a metadata-level relation claim;
- demonstrated textual dependence/correspondence requires inspection/collation.

The executable relationship contract is much weaker. `corpus/relationships.tsv` has open free-text `predicate`, `status` and `evidence_basis`; `tools/source_contract.py` provides strong structural checks for `part_of` and a targeted endpoint check for `textually_corresponds_to`, but it does not encode the semantic distinction between:

- “the finding aid says this is a copy/extract”;
- “inspection shows textual correspondence”;
- “evidence supports direct copying/dependence”;
- “relationship remains unresolved.”

The Wolkenburg corpus is expected to contain later copies/extracts of older Knau-related documents, so this is now a recurring real risk rather than an edge case.

## Failure modes

1. catalogue wording is stored as a strong `copy_of` claim and later read as demonstrated transmission history;
2. one agent uses `metadata_says_copy_of`, another `copy_of`, another free prose, with no predictable semantics;
3. generic `observed` status is interpreted as “observed in manuscript text” although the evidence was catalogue metadata;
4. textual correspondence is upgraded to direct copying without explicit adjudication;
5. a stronger relationship silently replaces a weaker historical claim instead of preserving evidence/status progression;
6. similar relations cannot be queried or reviewed consistently across Projects/Sources.

## Requirements implicated

- REQ-ARCH-005 — catalogue/finding-aid assertions, inference and direct inspection remain distinguishable;
- REQ-ARCH-006 — relationships are evidence-bearing claims, not identity shortcuts;
- D-005 — copy/extract/parallel/textual correspondence carry explicit evidence status;
- D-004 — archival, textual/palaeographic and historical truth domains do not silently rewrite each other;
- REQ-GWP-004 — consequential revision/upgrade remains reconstructable where material.

## Analysis required

Use real Wolkenburg examples plus the existing Reg. Ii 4/5 relation fixture to derive the **smallest** stable relation semantics that need deterministic protection.

Possible dimensions to test, without pre-authorizing a schema, include:

- relation predicate/family;
- evidence class or evidence level (catalogue metadata vs direct inspection/collation);
- claim/status strength;
- explicit supersession/upgrade path where consequential;
- source-relative loci where the relation is bounded.

Do not turn transmission history into a closed ontology and do not make code decide whether two manuscripts are actually copies.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] a catalogue-only copy/extract statement cannot be mistaken for demonstrated textual dependence;
- [ ] direct textual correspondence remains distinguishable from direct-copy/dependence claims;
- [ ] relation status/evidence semantics are consistent enough for a fresh agent/reviewer to interpret without old chat;
- [ ] stronger later adjudication can preserve/reconstruct the earlier weaker claim where consequential;
- [ ] relation endpoint/locus separation from #61 remains intact;
- [ ] existing Reg. Ii relationship rows remain losslessly representable;
- [ ] at least one Wolkenburg copy/extract fixture is tested;
- [ ] deterministic validation protects only stable semantics, not scholarly transmission judgement;
- [ ] any material shared contract implementation receives D-016 admission.

## Non-goals

- no universal transmission ontology;
- no automatic copy/dependence detection;
- no inference from similar wording or shared provenance alone;
- no replacement of independent witness reading/collation;
- no requirement to identify a lost original when evidence does not permit it;
- no implementation by this Issue alone.

## Wolkenburg relation refinement — common event/state must remain distinct from textual transmission

The Wolkenburg clarification in #270 adds a recurring relation class that this Issue must explicitly protect against accidental collapse: several family-property documents may describe the **same ownership state or transfer** while having no demonstrated textual dependence.

Keep at least these claims distinct:

```text
same / related historical event or ownership state
≠ same or similar wording
≠ textually corresponds to
≠ direct copy / extract / dependence
≠ structural part_of
```

This distinction already has a useful repository precedent: current `corpus/relationships.tsv` represents `belongs_to_event` separately from `textually_corresponds_to`, and the latter explicitly does not assert verbatim copying. Wolkenburg must preserve that separation when repeated transfers, successions or family-property descriptions recur across documents.

Transmission can also be non-linear:

- Document B may copy/extract A;
- A and B may both depend on an unidentified/lost exemplar C;
- B may report the same historical transfer as A without textual dependence;
- a later transcript can contain an older documentary layer while belonging materially to a later witness/source context.

Therefore relation strength/evidence must never be upgraded merely because subject matter, persons, dates or events overlap. Catalogue statement, direct textual correspondence, stronger dependence claim and unresolved relation remain separate evidential states; common-event relation is another distinct claim family.

Multiple dating layers are likewise not transmission proof: `same/similar text ≠ same production date`, and the date of the present witness must not be inherited from the represented act or event (REQ-HIST-011 / #115).

This makes #269 integral to the Wolkenburg concept, but still **fixture-driven**. Do not introduce a universal transmission ontology or make software decide copying/dependence. Deterministic protection should cover only stable relation/evidence semantics demonstrated by real cases. Any shared contract change remains D-016 material.
