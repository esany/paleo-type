## Domain context to persist and test

### A. Material/document-view continuity — primary for "same object?"

Before interpreting text, inspect whether adjacent/non-adjacent provider images are different views of the same physical piece.

Candidate cues to test against originals:
- matching folds, tears, stains, edge shapes, blank zones and paper proportions;
- repeated text fragments or seal/signature areas appearing under a different fold/overlay;
- front/reverse relationships;
- folded/unfolded or partial/full views;
- oversized-document coverage spread across several film/provider images;
- cord, seal-support, fold and overlap continuity;
- orientation/reversal/show-through consistent with a reverse side rather than another document.

The 0133–0135 correction is the known positive control: multiple provider images can represent one oversized charter.

### B. Layout / graphic articulation — primary for textual beginnings and endings

Explicitly inspect changes in:
- script size and care;
- enlarged or otherwise distinguished first line;
- initials / decorated opening;
- titulature or formal address;
- text-block width, indentation, margins and spacing;
- transition from dense running script to formal display layout;
- closing/date/signature blocks;
- seal/authentication zones;
- blank or deliberately reserved areas.

A distinguished first line / initial is a potentially strong internal beginning marker for formal instruments, but not a standalone boundary rule. It gains weight only in combination with other direct features and internal repetition within Nr. 632.

### C. Letters / administrative writings

For 18th-century letters and official writings test combinations such as:
- textual close + subscription/signature;
- folded sheet;
- blank/mostly blank outer page;
- exterior address / inscriptio;
- closure-seal remains;
- incoming, receipt, filing or other dorsal/registry annotations;
- concept/draft marks versus fair-copy/outgoing features.

A letter's reverse/address side belongs to the same object unless evidence shows otherwise. Exterior address, folds and closure evidence are therefore continuity markers rather than automatic new-document cues.

### D. Documentary / chancery stage

Where direct evidence permits, distinguish rather than collapse:
- concept / draft;
- fair copy / outgoing execution;
- received writing;
- copy / transcript;
- certification/endorsement;
- formal charter/instrument;
- petition/request;
- register/office annotation.

Do not identify stage from one visual cue or from catalogue expectations alone.

### E. Lehnssachen / 1704–1781 context

Use feudal/diplomatic context as a hypothesis generator only.

Potentially relevant document/functions include, where actually observed:
- Lehnsmutung / petition for investiture;
- Lehnspflicht-related material;
- Lehnbrief;
- Lehn(s)revers / Gegenbrief;
- related chancery, certification and registration material.

The existence of such forms in early-modern feudal practice does not prove they occur in Nr. 632. Same-fonds items may be used as contextual comparators but not as evidence for Nr. 632 content.

### F. Chronology / changing practice

Nr. 632 spans 1704–1781. Do not assume one invariant graphic or epistolary formula across the entire unit.

Use period change as a reason to test variation in:
- degree of formulaic opening/closing;
- letter ceremonial/layout;
- chancery notation;
- handwriting/display conventions;

not as permission to infer a date or genre without direct evidence.

### G. Archival order / reproduction order

The current Best. 20578 finding aid records later archival rearrangement of parts of the family archive, earlier unordered/loose material, and subsequent reorganization. It also states that the holdings were microfilmed for preservation and that the online digital images made from those microfilms became available in November 2025.

Therefore keep separate:

```text
historical documentary/business order
≠ later family/archive filing
≠ modern archival arrangement
≠ microfilm frame order
≠ provider-image sequence
```

Provider adjacency is useful evidence context, but never sufficient for documentary adjacency or boundary.

- material continuity;
- layout beginning/end signals;

- seal/authentication zones;

No fixed document ontology is required.
