## 1. Problem framing

The current repository is comparatively mature at executing, assuring, persisting and restarting an already-authorized research task. It is less mature at forming and comparing scientifically consequential candidate questions before a task is selected.

At the same time, several expensive failures were not simple domain mistakes. They arose when meaning or authority crossed a boundary incorrectly, for example:

- correspondence range → analysis unit;
- resource unavailable → task/roadmap blocker;
- technical PASS → implied scholarly adequacy;
- repository/history structure → present research priority.

A future solution therefore should not be only a better scholarly checklist and not only better software. It should combine:

1. question-relative scholarly competence;
2. explicit cross-disciplinary semantic/authority boundaries;
3. Human-Owner purpose/priority control;
4. AI for genuinely non-deterministic synthesis, hypothesis generation and interpretation;
5. software as an executable control plane for everything that can be represented, derived, validated or enforced deterministically.

## 2. Design hypothesis

The target should be a **generic, transdisciplinary, executable research system** in which:

- each discipline retains authority only for the claim types it can actually establish;
- no discipline, role or agent is permanent leader;
- leadership is claim-/question-relative;
- shared interface competence preserves meaning, uncertainty and authority across disciplinary and technical boundaries;
- the Human Owner controls purpose, priority, Project boundary and material change admission without being used as substitute specialist, repository synchronizer or software architect;
- AI assists where interpretation, synthesis, hypothesis generation or competing research framing is genuinely non-deterministic;
- deterministic software owns structured state, provenance, identity, dependency resolution, admissible state transitions, consistency, context assembly, regression and technical promotion controls wherever the semantics are stable enough to encode;
- generated status/context/restart views are derived from canonical owners instead of becoming additional hand-maintained truth stores.

The intended direction is **more executable semantics and less hand-maintained state prose**, not another documentation layer.

## 3. Four authority classes

A future design should keep at least four authority classes distinct.

### 3.1 Scholarly/domain authority

Qualified scholarly competence establishes domain claims within its evidential limits: e.g. graph identity, documentary structure, historical word meaning, source function, metrological interpretation, editorial consequence.

It does not silently decide Project priority or software architecture.

### 3.2 Human-Owner authority

The Human Owner controls research purpose, priority, Project boundary, consequential use/publication and material research-system admission.

The owner should receive a specialist-prepared decision surface rather than be required to perform missing disciplinary synthesis or technical design.

### 3.3 AI capability without durable authority

AI may generate hypotheses, competing research questions, synthesis, explanations, conflict/premise detection and candidate interpretations.

AI output is proposal/reasoning material until the required domain/owner authority admits a consequence. Model output must not become canonical truth merely because it is fluent, repeated or technically persisted.

### 3.4 Deterministic system authority

Software may enforce already-established semantics: identities, controlled state, provenance links, admissible transitions, dependency/authority resolution, derived context, consistency constraints, regression and promotion gates.

Software does not create historical meaning or scientific relevance. Its authority is over **faithful representation and enforcement of admitted semantics**, not scholarly truth.

## 4. Shared interface competence

Deep disciplinary expertise is not enough. The system also needs a small shared interface literacy so that one competence can hand work to another without semantic or authority laundering.

The cross-cutting interface competencies to analyse are:

1. **Epistemic-boundary literacy** — distinguish observation, reading, interpretation, hypothesis, decision and authority.
2. **Claim-ownership literacy** — know which claim types the current competence can establish and where its authority stops.
3. **Semantic-translation literacy** — preserve meaning when moving between disciplines, natural language, structured values and software.
4. **Uncertainty literacy** — preserve unresolved/conditional/alternative states rather than flattening them during handoff.
5. **Formalisation literacy** — recognise which distinctions are stable enough to encode without forcing research judgement into a false schema.
6. **Provenance literacy** — retain traceability from claim through evidence, transformation, review and decision.
7. **Automation-boundary literacy** — distinguish deterministic derivation from AI-assisted synthesis and specialist/Human judgement.
8. **Falsification/review literacy** — state what evidence or counterexample could change the claim or framing.

These are interface capabilities, not a proposal for eight new roles.

## 5. Claim-relative competency model

A future solution should activate competence by the claim/question rather than by a permanent role pipeline.

Illustrative allocations:

| Claim/question type | Normal lead competence | Important interface boundary |
|---|---|---|
| What graph/sign is written? | palaeography | semantic plausibility may suggest tests but may not manufacture graphemes |
| What is the physical/textual organization? | codicology/material study + diplomatics/editing as applicable | physical address/block is not automatically the analysis unit |
| What documentary object/process is present? | archival science / diplomatics / source-type history | archival structure is not Project priority |
| What does a historical word/form mean? | historical philology / lexicography | depends on evidentially supported reading |
| What does a numeral/unit/accounting sign mean? | notation/metrology/accounting history after sign reading | written sign identity and interpreted value/function remain separate |
| What institutional/legal/social function does a passage perform? | source criticism + relevant domain history, with diplomatics | contextual model remains constrained by text/layout evidence |
| How should an established distinction be represented computationally? | Requirements Engineering / information modelling / RSE | technical representation may not redefine domain semantics |
| Which research question is most consequential next? | transdisciplinary scholarly synthesis + research methodology/decision analysis | Human Owner retains purpose/priority authority |
| What Project purpose/priority/scope should be authorized? | Human Owner | specialists prepare the decision; owner does not substitute for missing specialist work |

A competence may lead one sub-question and be subordinate or review-only on another.

## 6. Additional competency families needed for the whole system

The solution concept requires more than historical source disciplines.

### 6.1 Research methodology / philosophy of inquiry

Needed for question formation, competing hypotheses, falsifiability, research design, evidence ceilings and the distinction between a merely unresolved point and a scientifically consequential uncertainty.

### 6.2 Decision science / value of information

Needed to compare candidate questions by scientific consequence, discriminating power, expected information gain, evidentiary feasibility, tractability, cost, uncertainty and Project fit without turning scientific priority into a mechanical score.

### 6.3 HCI / HITL / cognitive ergonomics

Needed to design decision surfaces that show the Human Owner the right trade-offs and evidence without turning the owner into a courier, dispatcher, specialist fallback or approval rubber stamp.

### 6.4 Requirements Engineering and scholarly-computational translation

Needed to determine which domain distinctions are stable/material enough to become system requirements, how to preserve them losslessly and how to prevent technical preferences from creating research requirements.

### 6.5 Software architecture / Research Software Engineering

Needed to build the executable control plane, resolvers, validation, APIs/CLI/UI, derived views, workflow integration and maintainable automation while keeping technical structure subsidiary to admitted scholarly semantics.

### 6.6 Information/data architecture

Needed to minimise duplicate truth stores, define canonical versus derived state, preserve identity/provenance and avoid allowing repository topology to become research semantics.

### 6.7 Verification engineering

Needed to convert stable semantic boundaries into executable invariants and regressions. Verification establishes system conformance, not scholarly truth.

### 6.8 AI systems / context engineering

Needed to assemble canonical context deterministically, route tools/evidence, expose provenance and prevent model/session context from becoming the implicit state owner.

### 6.9 Security / Git promotion governance

Needed to protect canonical promotion mechanically where appropriate. Promotion integrity remains separate from scholarly validation.

## 7. Functional research-framing layer

The largest current capability gap suggests a pre-execution framing layer with the following conceptual flow:

```text
Project purpose
+ current canonical state
+ source understanding
+ established claims
+ open uncertainties / evidence ceilings
+ permitted post-observation context
        ↓
question-relative competency activation
        ↓
multiple competing / complementary research frames
        ↓
transdisciplinary premise and consequence review
        ↓
comparative decision analysis
        ↓
Human-Owner decision surface
        ↓
explicitly authorized scholarly scope
        ↓
existing evidence/execution/review/persistence workflow
```

The framing layer should complement, not replace, the already-mature execution controls.

## 8. Candidate-question object as a derived decision surface

Before authorization, a candidate research question should be treated as a derived proposal, not canonical task authority.

A useful conceptual representation may include:

- question;
- why it matters;
- Project-purpose relation;
- current assumptions tested;
- expected scholarly consequence;
- discriminating power / expected information gain;
- implicated competencies and lead/contribute/review relation;
- required evidence;
- known evidence ceiling;
- tractability/cost;
- contamination or authority-laundering risk;
- what would change if answered;
- what would remain unchanged.

This is a design hypothesis for evaluation, not an admitted schema.

## 9. Software as executable control plane

Software should enter the solution from the beginning of concept design, not only after scholarly design is complete.

For every important semantic distinction the co-design questions should be:

```text
Is this epistemically necessary?
Is it generic enough to persist?
Can it be represented without semantic loss?
Can it be derived deterministically?
Can it be validated mechanically?
Does it require AI synthesis?
Where must specialist or Human judgement remain explicit?
```

Stable invariants should migrate from prose-only guidance into executable controls where technically and epistemically justified.

Illustrative invariants to evaluate:

```text
candidate_question != authorized_scope
resource_state != task_authority
source_address != analysis_unit
correspondence_range != analysis_unit
ci_pass != scholarly_acceptance
open_issue != project_priority
historical_artifact != current_truth_owner
generated_view != canonical_owner
AI_proposal != accepted_claim
```

The exact executable form requires separate requirements/design work.

## 10. Automation allocation hypothesis

### Prefer deterministic automation for

- Project/Source/File identity and resolution;
- evidence bindings and provenance traces;
- current state and gate resolution where semantics are controlled;
- current/historical/canonical/derived distinctions;
- dependency and authority lookup;
- allowed state-transition checks;
- context assembly from canonical owners;
- consistency/regression checks;
- generated restart/status/context views;
- promotion checks and repository hygiene;
- safe routine synchronization where one canonical owner already exists.

### Prefer AI assistance for

- competing hypothesis generation;
- candidate research-question generation;
- cross-disciplinary perspective synthesis;
- premise/conflict detection;
- interpretive candidate generation;
- relevance explanation;
- synthesis of heterogeneous literature/source information.

### Keep explicit specialist/Human judgement for

- source readings and domain interpretations where evidence requires expertise;
- scholarly adequacy and interpretation conflicts;
- scientific consequence where not mechanically reducible;
- Project purpose/priority/scope authorization;
- material research-system admission.

## 11. Information architecture direction: less hand-maintained Markdown

A future accepted architecture should aim to reduce duplicated hand-maintained state prose.

Conceptually separate:

1. **canonical structured state** for stable machine-actionable facts;
2. **scientific argumentation** for reasoning, interpretation, alternatives and evidence discussion that genuinely require prose;
3. **generated views** for status, restart context, dashboards, AI context and summaries.

The design should not convert all scholarship into schemas. The criterion is whether a distinction is stable, material and safely formalizable.

A useful architecture test is:

> If two hand-maintained files must repeat the same state fact to remain coherent, first ask whether that fact should have one structured owner and generated views instead.

The concept should therefore reduce, not expand, a Markdown state/documentation jungle.

## 12. Semantic handoff contract

Every consequential disciplinary or technical boundary should conceptually answer:

- what enters the handoff;
- what it means;
- what authority it currently has;
- what uncertainty accompanies it;
- what transformation is being performed;
- what may legitimately follow;
- what explicitly may not follow;
- which owner/competence receives the result;
- how semantic preservation can be reviewed or tested.

This need not become a new file/template family. It may later be represented through typed state, tooling or existing owners if requirements justify that implementation.

Example conceptual separation:

```text
palaeographic output:
  written sign: "80"
  authority: sign identity
  not established: value / unit / accounting function

metrological interpretation:
  candidate meaning: conditional
  requires: contextual/domain evidence
  does not alter: diplomatic reading
```

A future software control could prevent an interpretive candidate from silently overwriting the diplomatic reading owner.

## 13. Four analyses required before any implementation admission

This concept is not ready for development. At minimum, a later design phase should integrate four analyses on the same real fixtures.

### A. Epistemic / competency model

Determine claim classes, question-relative leadership, required contributing/review competencies, authority limits and the minimum shared interface literacy.

### B. Research-framing / decision model

Determine how source understanding becomes multiple candidate research questions, how specialists challenge their premises and how scientific consequence/information value is compared without producing a fake automatic score.

### C. Executable system model

Determine the smallest useful state model, canonical/derived boundaries, invariants, transitions, resolvers, validators and generated views. Explicitly identify existing owners that should be reused instead of creating new layers.

### D. Human / AI / software allocation

For each function classify the preferred mode:

`deterministic | AI-assisted | specialist judgement | Human-Owner decision`

and identify unsafe substitutions between modes.

## 14. Cross-fixture validation requirement

The concept must not be validated only on the Reg. Ii 5/Knau case.

Before generic promotion, evaluate it against at least:

1. the current Reg. Ii 5 fixture with its well-documented framing/authority failures;
2. a materially different historical source/project structure requiring different competencies;
3. a case where the highest-value next question does not originate in an unresolved transcription locus.

The goal is to test generic function without converting Knau/visitation-specific structure into universal architecture.

## 15. Explicit non-goals

This concept does **not** currently authorize:

- a permanent role or agent registry;
- a multi-agent orchestration system;
- a mandatory Research-Framing Markdown template family;
- a universal historical-source ontology;
- a database migration;
- a new workflow engine;
- automatic scientific scoring/ranking;
- replacement of specialist judgement with AI;
- replacement of Human-Owner priority authority;
- conversion of all scholarly prose into structured records;
- implementation of the illustrative invariants or candidate-question object;
- a new Project `NEXT ACTION`.

## 16. Success criterion for a later solution

A mature solution should be able to demonstrate that it can:

> derive several serious next research questions from rich but unprioritized scholarly state; activate the relevant competencies by claim/question rather than permanent hierarchy; expose premises, evidence needs, uncertainty and expected consequence; give the Human Owner a real scientific priority decision; and then execute the authorized scope while deterministic software preserves state, provenance, authority, consistency, restartability and promotion boundaries.

A complementary engineering criterion is:

> scientific expressiveness and auditability increase while less state must be manually restated, less canonical context must be reconstructed by AI and fewer semantic invariants depend on humans remembering prose rules.

## 18. Pre-synthesis evidence programme — concept refinement 2026-09-14

Refinement baseline: `main@3fa00c5dd52d0746c4c4584af3e1bed03dfcd737`.

The second-level landscape audit strengthens one conclusion before synthesis: **`requirement exists` and `requirement is operationally effective` are different states**. A large label such as `activation gap` is still too coarse to design against.

Before a solution synthesis, each consequential gap should therefore be reconstructed through the actual mechanism by which an already-known principle did or did not become effective.

### 18.1 Activation mechanics as a third analytical level

For each candidate gap, establish:

```text
what should have happened?
when should it have happened?
which information should have triggered it?
which competence should have recognized the trigger?
what output should that competence have produced?
which competence/system/owner should have consumed that output?
what authority should the output have carried?
what actually happened instead?
which part was observable mechanically?
which part could have been enforced mechanically?
which part required AI synthesis?
which part required specialist or Human judgement?
```

Distinguish at least:

```text
trigger not recognized
required information not visible
relevant competence unavailable
competence available but wrongly sequenced
correct output produced but not handed off
handoff changed meaning or authority
rule existed but had no operational mechanism
mechanism existed but no review/enforcement caught the breach
```

This refinement should prevent `activation` from becoming another catch-all root-cause label.

### 18.2 Guardrail versus productive capability

For each construction site, analyse both sides separately:

1. **negative protection** — which invalid inference/action must be prevented?
2. **positive capability** — what scientifically useful result must the system be able to produce?

Examples:

- A2: prevent addressability from creating priority **and** make relative scientific leverage among serious questions inspectable;
- B4: prevent context from manufacturing graphemes **and** reactivate context later for hypothesis/question generation;
- B5: prevent the Human Owner from being a substitute specialist **and** present an owner with genuinely specialist-prepared alternatives;
- D2: prevent fresh-session pseudo-independence **and** enable review to descend to the evidence depth needed for the disputed premise.

A future solution should not be judged adequate merely because it adds stronger prohibitions.

### 18.3 Earliest preventable point

For every demonstrated failure, identify the earliest stage where an already-valid requirement could realistically have prevented the error without relying on later clean-up.

This should distinguish whether prevention belongs primarily to:

- framing;
- source/object formation;
- competency activation;
- semantic handoff;
- owner decision;
- execution;
- review;
- persistence/propagation;
- technical promotion.

The aim is to avoid solving early semantic failures with unnecessarily heavy downstream machinery.

## 20. Common interface-analysis questions

Each scholarly and technical competence should answer the same small interface set so that synthesis can compare actual handoff needs without first imposing a universal ontology:

1. Which claim/question types can this competence establish or lead?
2. Which claim/question types can it explicitly not establish?
3. Which inputs are required, with what evidence/status constraints?
4. Which outputs does it produce?
5. Which uncertainty must survive the handoff?
6. What may legitimately follow from its output?
7. What must explicitly not follow?
8. At what stage/trigger should this competence become active?
9. When should it relinquish lead authority?
10. Which competence/system/owner normally consumes the output next?
11. What evidence or counterexample would falsify/change the output?
12. Which boundary facts are technically observable?
13. Which boundary conditions can be checked deterministically?
14. Where is AI synthesis useful but non-authoritative?
15. Where must qualified specialist or Human judgement remain explicit?

The resulting interface evidence may later justify typed contracts or tooling, but this analysis does not itself authorize a new template/schema family.

## 22. Cross-source genericity test

Reg. Ii 5 alone is insufficient for generic synthesis.

Before generic promotion, include materially different source structures, for example:

- an individual letter / narrative or person-centred source where argument, intention and correspondence context dominate;
- a serial accounting/administrative source where numerical, tabular or repetitive structure dominates;

## 23. Analysis protocol before concept challenge

To reduce confirmation bias, do not begin competence work by asking specialists to validate this concept.

Prefer two passes:

### Pass A — independent needs/evidence analysis

Provide the relevant canonical state, direct evidence owners and shared success/failure fixtures. Ask each competence to analyse needs, boundaries, failure mechanisms and positive capabilities without treating this concept candidate as a target architecture.

A fresh context is not sufficient evidence independence; consequential disputed premises must be traced to the appropriate direct evidence/owner depth.

### Pass B — concept challenge

Only after Pass A, compare the independent findings with this concept candidate:

- supported;
- missing;
- overformalized;
- redundant with an existing mature mechanism;
- Knau/source-type specific;
- unsafe authority transfer;
- unnecessary documentation/process;
- suitable for deterministic enforcement;
- necessarily judgement-based.

This should make the later synthesis evidence-driven rather than concept-confirming.

## 24. Synthesis-readiness criteria

Do not begin a material solution synthesis merely because the landscape is detailed. A synthesis is better justified when all of the following are available:

1. independent specialist/competency evidence for the low-maturity cluster A1/A5/B1/B2/B4/B5;
2. at least two materially different source fixtures beyond the current Reg. Ii 5 case;
3. activation-mechanics reconstruction for the major demonstrated failures/successes;
4. Requirements/RSE analysis separating normative gap, activation gap, mechanism gap and technical enforcement gap;
5. an explicit allocation for relevant functions across `deterministic | AI-assisted | specialist judgement | Human-Owner decision`;
6. HCI/decision evidence for what the Human Owner actually needs to choose between scholarly alternatives without specialist substitution or pseudoprecision;
7. evidence that proposed generic mechanisms do not duplicate already-mature protections such as resource-authority separation or Domain↔Dev meaning preservation.

At that point, the synthesis question becomes:

> Which smallest common mechanisms address several independently evidenced gap types while preserving mature existing controls and leaving irreducible scholarly judgement explicit?

Until then, further value should come primarily from competency research, cross-source fixtures, activation-mechanics reconstruction and formalizability analysis rather than from adding another internal taxonomy layer.
