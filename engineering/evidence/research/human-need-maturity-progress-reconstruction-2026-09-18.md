## 1. Executive reconstruction — why “maturity” entered the conversation

The maturity discussion did **not** begin because the repository lacked a scholarly method, because the Projects lacked a broad research purpose, or because the Human Owner wanted a new workflow.

It arose after a different system problem had already been substantially analysed and partially implemented:

1. `paleo-type` had accumulated a strong transdisciplinary scholarly method, scope-bound scholarly maturity gates, quality rules, authority boundaries, provenance/restartability infrastructure and deterministic repository support.
2. The system audit nevertheless showed a recurring operational failure: local safeguards and support work could be correct while the Human need and usable scholarly outcome were lost from view.
3. #225 was opened to operationalize the connection from Human need to scholarly outcome without turning the Human Owner into a specialist method designer or software/repository operator.
4. The first admitted implementation slice correctly solved a **negative authority problem**: it can expose that no current owner-authorized scholarly scope exists without manufacturing a next task.
5. Fresh repository state then showed the same practical situation across Reg. Ii 3, Reg. Ii 4 and Reg. Ii 5: all are established Projects with different histories and different levels/types of prior work, but none currently has an owner-selected active scholarly scope.
6. That correct “no active scope” result creates a genuine orientation problem for the Human Owner:
   - substantial scholarly and technical work has already happened;
   - that work is heterogeneous across Projects and across parts of each Project;
   - there is no simple current cursor;
   - the Human Owner should not have to reconstruct the repository's scholarly method, gate semantics, domain requirements and technical assurance state merely to understand what has been achieved and what further work would mean.
7. The idea of a “Reifegrad” entered as an attempted answer to that orientation problem:
   > Can the accumulated Project state be evaluated in a way that makes the real degree and quality of progress understandable and helps place possible next work in context?
8. The Human Owner rejected shallow answers because “maturity” here is not a label or a progress percentage. It is expected to become a **transparent, systematic, transdisciplinary explanation of the Project's present research state and the consequences of further work**.

Therefore the governing need is not “invent a maturity score”.

It is:

> **Make the accumulated research state of an established transdisciplinary Project cognitively and evidentially inspectable as a whole, while preserving the heterogeneous scope-bound truth underneath, so that the Human Owner can understand what has been done, how strong and complete it is, what remains open, and what different further work would change — without being required to reconstruct internal scholarly or technical machinery.**

“Maturity” is the language that exposed this need. It is not yet the final solution concept.

## 3. Human-need hierarchy

## 3.1 Research-outcome need

The highest-order need remains the repository's governing purpose:

> produce scientifically strong, evidence-constrained, usable understanding of historical manuscript sources.

The maturity/progress view is not itself the research product.

It is a **research-steering and understanding surface** that helps the Human Owner understand whether the Project is moving toward that product and what the accumulated work means.

## 3.2 Orientation / situational-awareness need

The Human needs to answer, for an established Project:

- What material belongs to the Project and what parts have actually been touched?
- What kinds of scholarly work have been performed on each relevant part?
- Which work is historical, current, reopened, superseded or merely preparatory?
- What concrete results were obtained?
- Which results are robust, provisional, unresolved or bounded by evidence?
- How much of the Project is represented by those results?
- Which parts remain essentially unworked?
- Where is the apparent “progress” only infrastructure/provenance preparation rather than deep scholarship?
- Where is a small scope deeply mature even though the Project as a whole remains largely unexplored?

This is more than status reporting. It is a **model of the current research situation**.

## 3.3 Decision-support need

The Human needs to understand possible further work in context:

- What specific deficiency, opportunity or maturity/quality dimension would a work block address?
- Is it deepening an already studied locus or widening Project coverage?
- Does it improve reading quality, source criticism, comparison, interpretation, consolidation, edition readiness, specialist validation, infrastructure reliability or human usability?
- What prerequisite evidence/competence does it require?
- What scholarly result could it plausibly produce?
- What would remain unchanged even if it succeeds?
- What later options would success enable?
- What work could become unnecessary?
- What evidence ceiling could stop it?

The Human should receive this contextualization **before** being asked to make a genuine priority decision.

## 3.4 Progress / maturity need

“How far are we?” means several different questions that must be kept separate:

1. **Breadth / coverage** — how much of the Project has been meaningfully touched?
2. **Depth of scholarly processing** — how far has a particular scope moved through the existing scholarly method?
3. **Acceptance/maturity** — which evidence-based maturity conditions have actually been reached for that scope?
4. **Quality** — how strong, complete, independent, source-critical and auditable is the work?
5. **Disciplinary adequacy** — were the scholarly competences relevant to the actual problem sufficiently engaged?
6. **Validation** — what has independent qualified specialist review?
7. **Infrastructure assurance** — how reliable/reproducible/restartable is the supporting research state?
8. **Usefulness** — what usable scholarly result is available now and what cannot yet responsibly be concluded?

A maturity view that answers only one of these is not adequate.

## 3.5 Transdisciplinary transparency need

The Human does not want a fixed checklist of disciplines.

The view must explain:

- which disciplinary perspectives were actually relevant to each material problem;
- why they were relevant;
- what they contributed;
- whether their applicable requirements were sufficiently addressed;
- where a discipline was correctly **not** activated;
- where an unaddressed relevant discipline creates a real research gap;
- where a specialist boundary exists.

This makes the transdisciplinary nature of the Project visible without turning it into a ritual “all disciplines complete” matrix.

## 3.6 Quality need

The Human must be able to distinguish:

- work done versus work well done;
- evidence inspected versus merely known to exist;
- representation completeness versus reading resolution;
- internally tested result versus independent corroboration;
- AI-/method-assisted result versus specialist-validated result;
- explicit uncertainty versus unresolved negligence;
- deep local result versus Project-wide coverage;
- technical consistency versus scholarly correctness.

Quality is therefore not a final badge. It is an independent explanatory layer across the whole map.

## 3.7 Technological/system assurance need

The technological state matters because the research must be trustworthy and usable.

The Human needs visibility into:

- exact provenance;
- evidence recoverability/inspectability;
- reproducibility;
- decision/history traceability;
- canonical ownership and avoidance of parallel truth;
- restartability without chat;
- semantic preservation across human/scholarly/technical representations;
- appropriate deterministic checks;
- AI versus scholarly versus specialist responsibility;
- human usability/auditability.

But this layer is **subsidiary**:
it cannot compensate for missing scholarly work and must never make a Project look scientifically mature merely because repository hygiene is strong.

## 3.8 Iteration need

The Human needs to understand research as **change in a multidimensional state**, not passage through a simple sequence.

For every meaningful work block the view should make visible:

- what the starting state was;
- which bounded scope was worked on;
- what scholarly work was actually performed;
- what new result or evidence ceiling occurred;
- which maturity/quality/coverage dimensions changed;
- which did not;
- what earlier claim was reopened, if any;
- what new possibilities became available;
- what work became unnecessary;
- what the resulting Project-level situation now looks like.

Iteration is therefore the bridge between **progress history** and **future decision support**.

## 3.9 Information-architecture / cognition need

The Human has repeatedly and explicitly rejected the equation:

```text
readable = simpler = fewer dimensions
```

The required meaning is:

> **Readability/transparency is the cognitive organization of the full relevant complexity.**

That requires:
- hierarchy;
- consistent axes;
- visual/structural comparison;
- whole/part navigation;
- progressive disclosure rather than information deletion;
- traceability from summary → scope → result → evidence;
- clear distinction of independent dimensions;
- explicit causal/semantic relationships;
- visible uncertainty and limits;
- understandable terminology at the human-facing layer;
- machine precision beneath the surface when required.

A short summary may be useful as an entry point, but it cannot replace the structured detail.

## 3.10 Delegation / burden need

The Human Owner should not have to infer from internal repository/method language:

- which scholarly activity comes next factually within an already chosen work block;
- which disciplinary method applies;
- which requirements are relevant;
- which evidence is missing;
- which specialist review is triggered;
- which technical tool/path/check is needed;
- how to reconstruct Project state from Git files.

The Human Owner retains genuine authority over:
- research purpose;
- material priority;
- material scope changes;
- non-derivable rights/authority facts;
- consequential publication/use;
- material research-system admission.

The maturity/progress view exists partly to prevent internal system complexity from being pushed upward as Human decision burden.

## 5. What “maturity” can and cannot mean

| Candidate meaning | Disposition | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Percentage complete | **Invalid as general Project maturity** | Implies one denominator, linearity and commensurable work where none exists |
| Highest gate reached | **Valid only for one explicitly evaluated scope** | Gates are evidence-based, scope-bound acceptance states |
| Current pipeline phase | **Invalid as maturity truth** | Existing method is iterative; later findings can reopen earlier work |
| Amount of work performed | **Useful history, not maturity** | Effort/activity does not establish scholarly adequacy |
| Publication readiness | **One possible outcome-specific state** | Relevant only where publication/edition is the intended outcome |
| Confidence | **Too narrow/unsafe alone** | Does not express coverage, evidence type, validation or maturity semantics |
| Project coverage/breadth | **Independent dimension** | Shows how much of the Project is represented, not how deeply |
| Scholarly depth | **Independent dimension** | Shows how far a bounded scope has been processed |
| Epistemic/scholarly quality | **Independent dimension** | Evaluates strength, uncertainty, independence and source criticism |
| Technological assurance | **Independent supporting dimension** | Enables trust/restart, not scientific maturity |
| Ability to support intended scholarly outcome | **Important contextual synthesis** | Depends on outcome and cannot replace underlying dimensions |
| Whole-Project maturity state | **Unsafe if treated as one scientific state** | Heterogeneous parts/scopes can be at different maturity levels |
| Project-level maturity/progress map | **Supported conceptual object** | Can aggregate/navigate heterogeneous scope-bound truths without flattening |

### Semantic definition

For this Human need:

> **Maturity is not one property of a Project. It is the evidence-grounded depth and adequacy of scholarly development of explicitly bounded parts of the Project, interpreted together with Project coverage, cross-cutting quality, disciplinary adequacy, validation, infrastructure assurance and outcome usefulness.**

The **Project-level view** is therefore a map/composition of these states, not a new maturity state itself.

## 6. Minimum independent dimensions

| Dimension | Human question | Assessment scope | What must remain distinct |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Project coverage** | Which parts of the Project have actually been worked on? | Project → source groups/loci | breadth ≠ depth |
| **Scholarly activity coverage** | What parts of the existing method were actually carried out? | bounded scope | activity ≠ acceptance |
| **Scope-bound scholarly maturity** | What scholarly maturity conditions are genuinely reached? | exact evaluated scope | local gate ≠ Project-wide maturity; gate ≠ priority |
| **Research result / outcome usefulness** | What usable knowledge/result exists now? | bounded result + intended outcome | result ≠ task completion |
| **Epistemic quality** | How well supported and critically tested is the result? | claim/result/scope | certainty ≠ quality; unresolved can be valid |
| **Disciplinary adequacy** | Were all relevant scholarly perspectives appropriately engaged? | problem/claim/scope | non-activation ≠ gap when irrelevant |
| **Specialist-validation state** | What has independent qualified validation? | consequential claim/scope | AI/method compliance ≠ specialist validation |
| **Research-infrastructure assurance** | Can the state be trusted, recovered and continued? | supporting evidence/data/system | technical quality ≠ scholarly maturity |
| **Human auditability/usability** | Can a researcher understand and challenge the state? | Project-level view + drill-down | readable ≠ simplified |
| **Iteration delta** | What changed because of this work block? | before/work/after | scholarly delta ≠ support delta |

### Interaction rule

These dimensions interact but are **non-substitutable**.

Examples:
- high technical assurance + no scholarly processing = technically reliable preparation, not advanced scholarship;
- deep G4 maturity on four folios + untouched remainder = deep local maturity with narrow coverage;
- broad diplomatic representation + weak internal analysis = broad representation but lower analytical maturity;
- unresolved reading after strong testing = valid mature uncertainty, not automatic deficiency;
- untriggered metrology in a text without consequential measure questions = correct non-activation, not a disciplinary gap.

## 7. Whole/part relationship

The Project-level surface must solve a hard information problem:

> How can the Human understand the Project “as a whole” without inventing a scientific maturity state for the whole?

### 7.1 Required hierarchy

```text
PROJECT
  ├─ known source groups / source nodes
  │    ├─ evaluated loci / bounded research scopes
  │    │    ├─ scholarly activities performed
  │    │    ├─ scope-bound maturity claims
  │    │    ├─ results / uncertainty / ceilings
  │    │    ├─ disciplinary adequacy
  │    │    └─ assurance / validation
  │    └─ prepared / historical / unactivated material
  └─ Project-level aggregate navigation
       ├─ coverage
       ├─ distribution of depth/maturity
       ├─ major quality/validation gaps
       ├─ historical/current/reopened distinction
       └─ possible work contextualized against the map
```

### 7.2 Aggregation principles

A Project-level view may aggregate only by preserving:

- exact scope of every scholarly maturity statement;
- denominator/context for any breadth statement;
- difference between preparation and scholarly processing;
- historical versus current state;
- reopened versus retained state;
- unresolved/evidence-ceiling states;
- local deep work versus broad shallow work;
- parts not evaluated at all.

It must not average or collapse semantically different dimensions into one score.

### 7.3 Reg. Ii 3 falsifier

A faithful Reg. Ii 3 view must simultaneously expose:

**Real prior work**
- Project authority established;
- two known internal source groups;
- exact Files registered;
- substantial provenance/crosswalk work;
- full Neustadt extent crosswalk with differentiated evidence levels;
- historical Knau localization preserved.

**Real absence/limits**
- no current owner-selected active scholarly scope;
- no current Project-relative gate progression;
- no full diplomatic representation for a current target;
- little current deep scholarly processing relative to the Project breadth;
- source-use register intentionally empty until actual Project-relative use exists.

Therefore:

- “Reg. Ii 3 = not evaluated” alone is false by omission;
- “Reg. Ii 3 = well advanced” is false by category error;
- “technical preparation complete” is also too strong because current-context evidence readiness is separate.

The required representation is **asymmetric maturity**:
broad/strong preparation in some support dimensions + low current depth in direct scholarly processing.

## 8. Iteration semantics

Iteration is not a new phase model.

For this Human need:

> **An iteration is a bounded work block inside the existing method that changes one or more parts of the Project-level maturity/progress map, followed by reassessment of the relevant whole.**

### Required before/after structure

```text
BEFORE
- exact scope
- existing activity/maturity/quality state
- evidence / assurance state

AUTHORIZED BOUNDED WORK
- scholarly objective
- required evidence/method/competence

DELTA
- scholarly result
- uncertainty/evidence ceiling
- support changes

AFTER
- changed dimensions
- unchanged dimensions
- reopened dimensions
- coverage effect
- new/removed dependencies
- newly enabled / unnecessary later work

PROJECT-LEVEL CONSEQUENCE
- how the local delta changes the understanding of the whole
```

### Scholarly delta versus support delta

A scholarly delta changes:
- evidence-based reading/interpretation;
- relevant uncertainty;
- source understanding;
- comparison/transmission understanding;
- maturity/acceptance state;
- bounded evidence ceiling.

A support delta changes:
- provenance mechanics;
- state synchronization;
- evidence access;
- tests;
- restartability;
- technical tooling.

Both can be valuable. They must never be reported as equivalent.

### Iteration patterns the view must support

1. **Depth increase without breadth increase**  
   One locus moves from representation into internal analysis.

2. **Breadth increase without depth increase**  
   A larger source group is mapped/represented shallowly.

3. **Quality increase without gate change**  
   Better provenance, adversarial review or specialist validation strengthens reliability.

4. **Reopen earlier maturity**  
   New evidence invalidates a previous reading/comparison.

5. **Evidence-ceiling iteration**  
   Work proves that current evidence cannot responsibly discriminate further.

6. **Negative/no-change result**  
   A hypothesis is tested and rejected, reducing future search space without “advancing a gate”.

Iteration is central because it explains **how research progress actually accumulates** rather than portraying the Project as a static dashboard.

## 9. Decision-support model

## 9.1 Decisions that should be derived/prepared by the system

Within an already authorized work purpose, the Human should normally receive prepared answers to:

- which existing methodological activity is required;
- which domain expertise is implicated;
- which evidence/prerequisite is needed;
- which formal requirement applies where stable/applicable;
- which specialist validation trigger exists;
- what repository/tool operation is needed;
- what a work block would change in the maturity/progress map;
- what it would not establish.

These are expert/system synthesis tasks, not routine Human choices.

## 9.2 Genuine Human-Owner decisions

The Human retains decisions about:

- material research purpose;
- priority among genuinely different valuable directions when not derivable;
- material scope changes;
- whether to stop/defer despite possible work;
- unresolved rights/authority facts;
- consequential publication/use;
- material research-system change admission.

## 9.3 Required decision preparation

Before asking a Human priority decision, the system should provide, as applicable:

- current Project-level map;
- relevant scope(s);
- demonstrated results and limits;
- why the decision is now needed;
- materially different work options;
- expected scholarly effect of each;
- prerequisites;
- disciplinary/specialist implications;
- evidence ceilings/falsifiers;
- what each option leaves untouched;
- later possibilities opened or removed.

This is decision support, not automatic ranking.

## 11. Requirements hierarchy

## Governing Human need

The Human Owner needs a systematic, transparent and cognitively navigable Project-level representation of accumulated transdisciplinary research progress that preserves scope-bound scholarly truth, explains quality and evidence, shows disciplinary and technical assurance, and contextualizes further work by its consequences — so that genuine research decisions can be made without requiring the Human to reconstruct internal method, gate logic or repository mechanics.

## Primary Requirement 1 — Whole-Project situational awareness without whole-Project maturity fiction

The surface shall represent the relevant Project as a whole while preserving heterogeneous states of its parts.

Supporting requirements:
- expose known source groups/loci and their research/work history;
- distinguish current, historical, reopened, superseded, preparatory and unactivated states;
- show exact scope for every maturity statement;
- show untreated/unevaluated areas rather than hiding them;
- never flatten heterogeneous scopes into one scientific maturity value.

## Primary Requirement 2 — Method-grounded progress trace

The surface shall show what scholarly work was actually performed using the existing repository METHOD.

Supporting requirements:
- distinguish activities from maturity/acceptance;
- link activity to exact scope;
- state concrete result, not just activity completion;
- show iteration history where materially relevant;
- distinguish scholarly delta from support delta.

## Primary Requirement 3 — Evidence-based maturity and quality

The surface shall explain how strong and mature each relevant scoped result is.

Supporting requirements:
- use existing scope-bound maturity semantics;
- expose evidence basis and inspection level;
- expose completeness for evaluated scope;
- preserve uncertainty/evidence ceilings;
- expose independent challenge/corroboration;
- preserve observation/reading/normalization/identification/interpretation separation;
- expose result → evidence traceability.

## Primary Requirement 4 — Dynamic transdisciplinary adequacy

The surface shall show whether the scholarly disciplines applicable to each problem were adequately engaged.

Supporting requirements:
- applicability is source/problem/claim-relative;
- explain why a discipline is relevant;
- show actual contribution/result;
- distinguish correctly untriggered domains from real gaps;
- expose where unresolved specialist judgement matters.

## Primary Requirement 5 — Specialist-validation transparency

The surface shall distinguish:
- method-/AI-assisted work;
- scholarly adequacy review;
- qualified specialist validation;
- Human/governance decisions.

No generic “validated” label may collapse them.

## Primary Requirement 6 — Research-infrastructure assurance as a separate support layer

The surface shall expose relevant support assurance without representing it as scholarly maturity.

Supporting requirements:
- provenance;
- exact evidence identity/access;
- reproducibility;
- canonical ownership/data integrity;
- decision history;
- restartability;
- semantic integrity;
- deterministic assurance where appropriate;
- AI/Human/specialist boundary;
- rights/access constraints.

## Primary Requirement 7 — Outcome usefulness and limits

The surface shall state what usable scholarly result currently exists and what stronger claims are not established.

Supporting requirements:
- explicit outcome/result;
- residual uncertainty;
- evidence ceiling;
- unresolved prerequisite/specialist dependency;
- no task-completion laundering.

## Primary Requirement 8 — Iteration/change intelligibility

The surface shall make research progress visible as change over time.

Supporting requirements:
- before state;
- exact work scope;
- scholarly/support delta;
- after state;
- changed/unchanged/reopened dimensions;
- coverage effect;
- consequences for later work.

## Primary Requirement 9 — Contextualized possible next work

When further work is relevant to a Human decision, the surface shall locate each option in the current maturity/progress map.

Supporting requirements:
- exact gap/opportunity addressed;
- affected scope/dimensions;
- prerequisites;
- expected scholarly delta;
- quality/maturity/coverage effect;
- what remains unchanged;
- evidence ceiling;
- later work enabled/removed;
- no automatic priority/ranking.

## Primary Requirement 10 — Cognitive transparency without complexity loss

The human-facing representation shall organize full relevant complexity rather than simplify it away.

Supporting requirements:
- consistent information hierarchy;
- comparison across independent dimensions;
- whole → part → evidence navigation;
- progressive disclosure;
- terminology understandable without internal implementation knowledge;
- exact machine-facing precision available beneath the human surface;
- no need to infer relationships from unstructured prose.

## Primary Requirement 11 — Human decision burden only where genuinely non-derivable

The system shall prepare scholarly/technical sequencing and present only true Human-authority decisions.

Supporting requirements:
- no requirement to know internal gate jargon;
- no requirement to choose repository paths/tools;
- no requirement to act as substitute specialist;
- explain why a Human decision is needed;
- provide the evidence/context needed to make it.

## Primary Requirement 12 — Derived/restartable view, not parallel truth

Any eventual view shall be rebuildable from existing canonical owners plus legitimate scholarly reasoning and shall not become a new manually maintained research-state store.
