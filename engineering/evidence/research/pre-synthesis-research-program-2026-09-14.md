## 1. Why this Research exists

The repository has reached an asymmetrical maturity state.

It is comparatively strong at executing, assuring, persisting and restarting an already-authorized research task. It is substantially less empirically mature at understanding how consequential research questions are formed, how relevant scholarly competencies interact before scope selection, how existing Requirements become effective or ineffective in practice, and which of the observed failures/generalizations survive materially different source and Project structures.

This programme is motivated by the existing evidence chain:

- `requirements/research-framing-transdisciplinary-competency-audit-2026-09-14.md` — reconstructs the earlier transdisciplinary Domain Review / research-sequencing function, shows that its authority boundaries remain valuable, and finds that pre-task scholarly framing became less visible after lean-down;
- `requirements/research-system-problem-landscape-2026-09-14.md` — maps the current construction sites and shows that many are not obviously missing-Requirement problems but may instead involve activation, sequencing, mechanism, visibility, freshness/propagation, function loss or technical enforcement;
- `projects/ega-reg-ii-5-knau/research/post-166-research-framing-review-2026-09-14.md` and the #165/#166/#169 correction history — provide the richest current Project fixture for framing, authority and analysis-unit failures/corrections;
- historical #71–#79, especially #73 and #79 — show that transdisciplinarity was previously modelled as competence-/authority-sensitive scholarly sequencing rather than as a permanent role ontology, and that competency breadth does not imply role proliferation;
- `requirements/executable-transdisciplinary-research-system-concept-idea-2026-09-14.md` — preserves a deliberately non-normative, rich solution hypothesis which is useful later but is already more detailed than the empirical Research base currently warrants.

The immediate Research problem is therefore not lack of internal terminology. It is the gap between **analytical/conceptual maturity and empirical validation**.

## 2. Research boundary

Research may:

- reconstruct what was actually observed;
- distinguish observations, interpretations and hypotheses;
- compare success/failure/correction/open-framing cases;
- investigate Requirement efficacy without presuming a new Requirement;
- examine fachliche independence and transdisciplinary interaction;
- identify alternative explanations and counterexamples;
- analyse timing, sequencing and dependency provenance;
- compare heterogeneous source/Project fixtures;
- use external disciplinary literature to challenge and contextualize findings;
- cautiously generalize when evidence recurs.

Research does **not** yet decide:

- the final generic capability map;
- new normative Requirements;
- a permanent role/agent/workflow model;
- a candidate-question schema;
- a semantic-handoff schema;
- a Human/AI/software target allocation;
- a state machine, data model or canonical schema family;
- a final solution architecture;
- implementation admission.

The epistemic transition out of Research occurs only when a later decision asks what the generic system **must** provide or how it **should** be built.

Descriptive generalisation is therefore distinct from normative promotion.

## 3. Current hypotheses must remain falsifiable

The existing audits and Concept Candidate provide useful hypotheses, but the primary Research must not be designed to confirm them.

Two symmetric distortion risks are especially important.

### 3.1 Concept Bias

```text
pre-existing conceptual frame / solution vocabulary
→ shapes what researchers look for
→ later findings are expressed through the same frame
→ the concept gains apparent empirical support
```

The Concept Candidate must therefore not serve as the primary theoretical frame for evidence collection.

### 3.2 Decomposition Bias

```text
bias protection through excessive disciplinary isolation
→ problem-relevant interaction/context is removed
→ each competence receives an artificial fragment
→ later synthesis attempts to reconstruct interaction that real research needed during inquiry
```

The programme must therefore not equate independence with isolation.

Working Research principle:

> **Preserve epistemic independence where needed; preserve transdisciplinary interaction where scientifically needed; make dependencies observable.**

Short form:

> **independence without isolation — interaction without authority laundering**

This is a Research-design guardrail, not a new METHOD rule.

## 4. Unit of investigation: scientific problem / fixture, not competence list

The listed competency families are an available **competence space**, not a set of mandatory roles or runs.

The concrete problem/fixture determines which perspectives become materially relevant, in which order and with what interaction.

Valid Research outcomes include:

- independently examinable within one competence;
- sequentially dependent on another competence;
- only sensibly examinable through transdisciplinary interaction;
- materially not applicable;
- unresolved because the available evidence cannot support the needed judgement.

No competence is activated merely for completeness.

A single investigation may legitimately move between more independent observation, sequential interaction and joint problem-level review. The Research-relevant information is why the mode changed and what epistemic effect followed, not whether one organizational mode was used consistently.

## 5. Minimal evidence discipline

Primary Research findings should preserve epistemic status without forcing architecture concepts into the record.

At minimum distinguish:

- **direct observation / evidence**;
- **fachliche interpretation**;
- **alternative explanation**;
- **hypothesis** where interpretation exceeds direct support;
- **uncertainty / evidence ceiling**;
- **counterexample or evidence that would change the judgement**;
- **tentative scope / generalisation status**.

Primary records should use the natural language of the relevant discipline where possible.

They should not be forced initially into fields such as `claim object`, `handoff`, `consumer`, `state transition`, `deterministic check` or `AI contribution` unless those categories independently arise from the evidence.

## 6. Dependency provenance

For material findings, preserve enough provenance to reconstruct the epistemic dependency structure under which the judgement was formed.

Proportionally record:

- evidence accessible at the time of judgement;
- already-known findings from other competencies;
- relevant disciplinary interactions;
- premises inherited but not independently checked;
- claims independently checked;
- changes to question, evidence search or investigation direction caused by another competence;
- intentionally withheld context where a contamination-sensitive stage required it.

This provenance is not a new fixed schema or workflow definition. It exists so later Research can distinguish, for example:

- genuine independent checking;
- legitimate sequential dependency;
- productive joint inquiry;
- premise inheritance;
- early convergence;
- context starvation.

## 7. Primary-first, secondary-analysis-later protocol

### 7.1 Primary fixture analysis

Begin with direct/canonical evidence and a fachlich open question.

Ask primarily:

- what is actually supported here?
- what fachlich relevant process, problem, success or limit is visible?
- what alternative explanations exist?
- what cannot be concluded?
- what evidence would change the conclusion?
- what appears fixture-bound and what might transfer?

Do not start by asking the competence to validate:

- the Concept Candidate;
- a predetermined capability model;
- fixed gap categories;
- a Human/AI/software allocation;
- a preselected interaction model.

### 7.2 Freeze primary findings

Primary findings must remain traceable after later extraction and synthesis.

The persistent Research evidence must distinguish conceptually between:

```text
A. original/open primary finding
B. later structured extraction
C. cross-competence interpretation
D. cross-fixture/cross-source interpretation
E. Concept Challenge
```

Later integration may interpret A; it must not silently rewrite A so that it fits the current taxonomy.

### 7.3 Secondary hypothesis testing

Only after primary findings are sufficiently stable may the Research explicitly test existing analytical hypotheses such as:

- activation failure;
- sequencing/timing failure;
- mechanism gap;
- visibility/discoverability gap;
- freshness/propagation gap;
- function loss after simplification;
- technical enforcement gap;
- translation/authority error;
- local versus interaction/composition failure;
- protective guardrail versus productive scholarly ability.

These are comparison hypotheses, not mandatory classifications.

## 8. Time and hindsight discipline

Failure analysis must not assume that a later-corrected error was always preventable with the evidence available earlier.

Where relevant distinguish:

```text
earliest detectable point
earliest preventable point
earliest recoverable point
```

and explicitly allow:

```text
no earlier prevention justified by evidence available at the time
```

This prevents retrospective knowledge from being projected into earlier research states.

## 9. Existing Requirement efficacy

The Level-2 landscape suggests that Requirement existence and operative efficacy must be separated.

For materially implicated existing Requirements, investigate proportionally:

```text
Requirement exists?
→ trigger recognizable?
→ required information visible?
→ relevant competence available?
→ competence activated at an appropriate time?
→ output produced?
→ meaning / uncertainty / authority preserved?
→ correct consumer or consequence?
→ operational mechanism available?
→ review/enforcement available?
→ desired global scholarly result achieved?
```

Possible Research findings include:

- genuine normative gap;
- Requirement present but not activated;
- timing/sequencing problem;
- mechanism gap;
- visibility/freshness problem;
- translation/authority error;
- enforcement gap;
- function loss;
- several locally valid mechanisms combining into an inadequate global state;
- an apparent composition problem that reduces to one concrete incorrect translation;
- insufficient evidence to decide.

Do not promote these analytical outcomes into a new controlled Requirement taxonomy during this programme.

## 10. Guardrails and positive scholarly abilities

The current repository is strong at many negative protections. Research must also investigate what productive scholarly work actually requires.

Examples of open empirical questions include:

- not only how addressability must be prevented from creating priority, but how consequential research alternatives are actually formed;
- not only how context is prevented from manufacturing source readings, but when and how context becomes productively necessary after contamination-sensitive observation;
- not only how Human-Owner specialist substitution is prevented, but what evidence and alternatives the Owner actually needs for a purpose/priority decision;
- not only how review avoids premise inheritance, but what evidence depth different claim types actually require;
- not only whether disciplinary boundaries are preserved, but how cross-disciplinary interaction changes the scientific question or evidence strategy.

A phrase such as `positive capability` remains a Research shorthand only. The existence of a productive scholarly practice does not yet prove that the generic system should encode it as one capability.

## 14. External literature protocol

External fachliche literature and standards are used to challenge, contextualize and extend fixture findings, not to preload a fashionable framework.

Preferred sequence:

```text
direct fixture analysis
→ provisional interpretation / uncertainty
→ external literature
→ comparison / challenge / expansion
```

For material external claims record:

- complete reference;
- concrete supported claim;
- epistemic role (`empirical`, `methodological`, `normative`, `technical standard`, `analogical`);
- relevance to the fixture/question;
- contradictory or limiting evidence where available.

Engineering frameworks may help analyse system phenomena but do not acquire scholarly authority over historical interpretation.

## 15. Evidence strength and generality

- within-project recurrence;
- cross-competence convergence on one fixture;

- cross-source recurrence;
- provisional generic hypothesis;
- source-/purpose-specific or contradicted finding;
- insufficient evidence.

This programme should not normally claim `strong generic evidence` from a small intentionally heterogeneous sample.

## 22. Completion criteria for the Research phase

Research is decision-ready when the central areas can answer, proportionally to available evidence:

- what was actually observed?
- which mechanism is sufficiently supported?
- what alternative explanations remain?
- what fachliche needs or productive scholarly practices appear?
- which existing Requirements were relevant and how did they actually function?
- what did independence or interaction contribute or distort?
- how far does the result travel beyond one fixture, one Project and one source type?
- what counterexamples exist?
- what remains unknown?
- what aspects of the Concept Candidate gained evidence, lost plausibility or remain unsupported?

The Research phase is **not** complete merely because all competency names have been visited or because a large synthesis document exists.

Breadth must not substitute for fachliche depth.

`not materially applicable`, `insufficient evidence` and `no generic conclusion justified` are valid Research outcomes.
