## Method

Execute the following stages in order.

---

## Stage 1 — Reconstruct the problem chronology

Produce a chronological reconstruction of **why the topic of maturity appeared**.

Do not start at the word “Reifegrad”.

Trace the chain from the higher-level system problem.

At minimum ask:

1. What larger practical problem was the Human Owner trying to solve before maturity was discussed?
2. What had already been completed in the system/repository?
3. What decision or orientation problem remained?
4. Why did the current absence of active Project work matter?
5. Why was a maturity/progress view proposed as useful?
6. What did the Human Owner expect that view to make understandable?
7. At what points did the assistant change the problem definition?
8. Which Human corrections reveal the real requirement most clearly?

Output a timeline with:
- trigger;
- Human need;
- assistant interpretation;
- Human correction;
- requirement learned.

---

## Stage 2 — Separate the levels of need

Do not collapse “need” into one sentence.

Classify at least:

### 2.1 Research-outcome need
What scholarly outcome does the Human ultimately want from the Projects/system?

### 2.2 Orientation / situational-awareness need
What must the Human understand about current Project state before choosing or authorizing further work?

### 2.3 Decision-support need
What decisions must the Human genuinely make, and what analysis should the system prepare before presenting those decisions?

### 2.4 Progress / maturity need
What does “how far are we?” actually mean in this context?

### 2.5 Transdisciplinary transparency need
What must be visible about the contribution/adequacy of different scholarly domains?

### 2.6 Quality need
What must be visible about scientific reliability, evidence, validation and limitations?

### 2.7 Technological/system assurance need
What must be visible about provenance, reproducibility, restartability, semantics, AI boundaries and technical reliability?

### 2.8 Iteration need
What must the Human understand about how one work block changes the Project state and what becomes possible afterward?

### 2.9 Information-architecture / cognition need
What does the Human mean by readability/transparency?

Explicitly test the proposition:

> readability/transparency is not simplification of complexity; it is cognitively effective organization of the full relevant complexity.

### 2.10 Delegation / burden need
Which specialist/method/technical sequencing decisions must **not** be pushed back to the Human Owner?

## Stage 3 — Reconstruct the “job to be done”

Write the core job in ordinary research-facing language.

It must include:
- the situation in which the Human invokes the view;
- what the Human is trying to understand;
- what decision/action becomes possible afterward;
- what must remain inspectable/challengeable;
- what must not be hidden behind technical labels.

Then write at least three subordinate jobs:
1. understand what has been done;
2. assess how strong/complete it is;
3. understand the consequence of possible next work.

Do not prescribe implementation.

## Stage 7 — Define iteration semantically

Do not assume iteration means “next phase”.

Reconstruct from the Human need what an iteration must make visible.

At minimum:

```text
BEFORE
→ authorized bounded scholarly work
→ scholarly/support delta
→ AFTER
→ changed / unchanged / reopened dimensions
→ consequence for the Project-level map
→ resulting possibilities / ceilings
```

Clarify:
- what counts as scholarly delta;
- what counts only as support delta;
- whether an iteration can deepen one locus without changing breadth;
- whether it can expand breadth without increasing depth;
- whether it can reopen earlier maturity;
- whether it can end in an evidence ceiling;
