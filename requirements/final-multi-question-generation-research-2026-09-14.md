# Part A — Reg. Ii 5 question-generation fixture

## 3. Starting state

The locus already supports a coherent set of economic/institutional observations: monetary and natural obligations, holdings, parish wood, substitution/compensation, temporary use/return, property replacement, `pfarrrecht` and a separately headed annual `Zulage`.

The current source understanding explicitly separates stable source addresses from functional units and leaves local objects, quantities and the two written `80` signs unresolved where evidence is insufficient.

The Project has no owner-selected active scholarly scope.

## RF191-R1 — how are the recorded Knau parish resources and obligations organized as an institutional/economic arrangement?

### Question

> How do the recorded money, natural produce, land, wood, substitution/compensation and replacement obligations fit together as a documented arrangement for the material support and resource rights of the parish/priest at Knau?

### Why this question emerged

The source-understanding synthesis no longer looks like a collection of isolated unclear loci. It exposes a repeated relation between parish income, land/resource rights, temporary substitution and recurring obligations across several separately bounded rules.

The question was generated by the **co-occurrence of several already-established functions**, not by one unresolved word.

### Why scientifically interesting

It can test whether the locus documents a coherent local institutional/economic arrangement or merely juxtaposes unrelated obligations. That distinction affects how the visitation record can be used for parish, property and institutional history.

### Discriminating / advancing evidence

- wider Reg. Ii 5 entries for the same parish and neighbouring parishes;
- other securely comparable visitation records in the same administrative setting;
- the controlled Reg. Ii 4 witness where witness relationship is secure;
- archival/diplomatic evidence about the recording purpose and structure of the visitation;
- source-type/domain scholarship on parish property, benefice income and visitation recording.

### Boundary

The question must remain about **recorded institutional arrangements** unless independent evidence supports claims about actual practice. A visitation/control record cannot be treated as transparent observation of lived compliance.

### Disciplinary challenge

Visitations scholarship supports the scientific relevance of rights, property, income, church goods and local institutional arrangements. It simultaneously weakens any formulation that would infer actual everyday practice directly from the recorded norm or report.

**Disposition:** serious and source-type credible if phrased as a source-critical institutional/economic question.

### Dependency provenance

Generated from the source-wide functional synthesis plus source-type knowledge that visitation files record rights/property/income. The external scholarship narrowed the claim from `how the parish economy worked` to `what institutional/economic arrangement the visitation records and how far other evidence supports practice`.

## RF191-R2 — do the substitution/replacement rules instantiate a shared resource-maintenance logic?

### Question

> Are the wood-substitution, temporary use/return and field/replacement rules separate local arrangements only, or do they evidence a recurring principle for maintaining parish resources and compensating temporary loss of use?

### Why this question emerged

#166 prevents merging adjacent rules into one unit, but the wider locus contains multiple securely separate rules involving `dargegen`, temporary replacement, return/restoration and another field.

The question therefore asks about **relation among distinct rules**, not whether they are one text unit.

### Why scientifically interesting

A repeated compensation/replacement logic could illuminate how parish resource rights were institutionally protected. Failure to find such recurrence would instead reinforce the independence of the local rules.

### Discriminating / advancing evidence

- exact relation of `62v-L08–25` property clauses where evidentially recoverable;
- comparable resource/property clauses elsewhere in Reg. Ii 5;
- parallel visitation material in controlled related records;
- legal/institutional context only after source-side recurrence is established.

### Boundary

The thematic similarity of adjacent rules is not evidence that they share one legal mechanism. The previous #165 over-grouping is a direct warning against promoting convenient relation into unit or function identity.

### Disciplinary challenge

The source type makes property/right questions legitimate, but the challenge rejects a pre-labelled `resource-maintenance system` unless recurrence can be shown beyond this compact cluster.

**Disposition:** serious comparative hypothesis; deliberately falsifiable and not established by proximity alone.

### Dependency provenance

Generated from the corrected #166 separation plus the recurring replacement/compensation language. The earlier analysis-unit failure materially changed the formulation: relation is investigated without collapsing unit boundaries.

## RF191-R3 — what can be established about the composition of parish income before exact quantitative reconstruction?

### Question

> What qualitative composition of parish income and obligations is already supported across cash, grain, land/resource and householder payments, and which additional quantitative conclusions genuinely depend on resolving notation/measure questions?

### Why this question emerged

The current state already distinguishes several income/obligation forms while explicitly preserving unresolved quantity signs and measures.

### Why scientifically interesting

It separates a potentially useful institutional/economic conclusion from a false requirement that every numeral or measure be normalized first. It also tests where the present evidence ceiling actually affects historical inference.

### Discriminating / advancing evidence

- established categories and counterparties across the locus;
- targeted metrological/notational work only for quantities that materially alter composition or comparison;
- wider visitation entries for comparative proportions only after normalization is defensible.

### Boundary

No total, proportion or normalized value may be manufactured from unresolved signs. A qualitative composition question is currently better supported than a quantitative reconstruction.

### Disciplinary challenge

Visitation scholarship confirms that clerical income, lands, natural dues and money payments are characteristic evidential content. The challenge therefore strengthens the qualitative question while treating precise economic calculation as a later evidence-dependent branch.

**Disposition:** serious and currently tractable at qualitative level; quantitative version is evidence-limited.

### Dependency provenance

Generated by interaction between source-understanding and metrological/evidence-boundary awareness. The unresolved notation changed the level of the question rather than automatically creating a notation task.

# Part B — Darwin correspondence question-generation fixture

## 4. Starting state

Letter 5098 is short and textually uncomplicated, but one headline fact remains conjectural: `23 May [1874 or 1875?]`. The edition explicitly derives the range relationally from a later 1876 letter. The photograph is missing from the Darwin Archive, and the letter survives in Copenhagen tipped into a copy of `Orchids`. Steenstrup had earlier supplied specimens for Darwin's cirripede work.

## RF191-D1 — can the year be narrowed responsibly, and which evidence would actually discriminate 1874 from 1875?

### Question

> Can the year of Letter 5098 be narrowed beyond `[1874 or 1875?]`, and which independent chronological/material clues would discriminate the two possibilities?

### Why this question emerged

The edition itself marks the year as conjectural and identifies the later 1876 relation as the current basis.

### Why scientifically interesting

It is a concrete editorial/source-critical question with a clear uncertainty and possible discriminating evidence. A firmer date can improve chronology and contextual interpretation without changing the letter text.

### Discriminating / advancing evidence

Following the Darwin editors' own documented practice:

- dated incoming/outgoing Steenstrup correspondence;
- replies and references to photograph exchange;
- postal-address history;
- paper, watermark, handwriting or recipient endorsement if the original permits;
- dated events/publications only where genuinely linked to the item.

### Boundary

The present `a year or two ago` relation does not distinguish 1874 from 1875 by itself. The question is responsible precisely because it identifies the current evidence ceiling rather than guessing.

### Disciplinary challenge

The Darwin Correspondence Project explicitly identifies letter dating as a difficult inferential task and lists exactly these evidence classes. This independently supports the question's editorial credibility.

**Disposition:** strong serious question within the editorial purpose of the fixture.

### Dependency provenance

Generated directly from the edition's uncertainty marker. Editorial policy expanded the evidence search but did not change the underlying question.

---

## RF191-D2 — does resolving the year materially change historical interpretation, or mainly editorial chronology?

### Question

> What historically consequential interpretation, if any, would differ between an 1874 and an 1875 date for this photograph exchange?

### Why this question emerged

A resolvable uncertainty is not automatically a high-value research problem. The letter's content is only thanks for a photograph, so the consequence of date refinement itself needs examination.

### Why scientifically interesting

It tests whether editorial precision changes a larger history-of-science or network interpretation, or whether the uncertainty can remain bounded without further work.

### Discriminating / advancing evidence

- Darwin–Steenstrup correspondence/activity in 1874 and 1875;
- timing of their scientific exchanges/publications;
- provenance/history of the photograph exchange;
- whether either year intersects a materially different episode in their relation.

### Boundary

Absence of consequential difference would be a valid terminal result and would weaken the priority of further dating work.

### Disciplinary challenge

The Darwin editorial policy justifies dating work, but does not imply that every uncertain date is equally historically consequential. This question survives precisely because it asks the consequence rather than presuming it.

**Disposition:** serious historical-method question; may terminate with `no material interpretive difference`.

### Dependency provenance

Generated by combining the direct uncertainty with the upstream Research rule that unresolved detail does not itself manufacture priority.

## RF191-D3 — what does this apparently minor exchange reveal about the longer Darwin–Steenstrup scientific relationship?

### Question

> How does the photograph exchange fit within the longer Darwin–Steenstrup relationship of specimen exchange, scientific correspondence and mutual recognition?

### Why this question emerged

The letter's footnote connects Steenstrup to earlier specimen supply for Darwin's cirripede research; the letter itself marks the photograph as highly valued.

### Why scientifically interesting

A minor social/material exchange may illuminate persistence and form of a scientific relationship after its earlier research collaboration.

### Discriminating / advancing evidence

- full Darwin–Steenstrup correspondence across the cited volumes;
- records of specimen exchange and Darwin's cirripede work;
- Steenstrup papers/provenance in Copenhagen;
- history of scientific photograph exchange only if evidence connects it to this pair.

### Boundary

This single short letter cannot establish the nature or importance of the relationship. The question is corpus-level and requires a broader evidence base.

### Disciplinary challenge

The Darwin edition itself points to earlier specimen relations, so the question is evidence-anchored rather than invented from generic network language. Its breadth must nevertheless remain proportional to the surviving correspondence.

**Disposition:** serious if the research purpose is history of scientific relations; not automatically the next editorial task.

### Dependency provenance

Generated by editorial footnote context. This is an example where permitted contextual knowledge creates a new historical question rather than establishing a claim about the letter itself.

# Part C — Washington cash-account question-generation fixture

## 5. Starting state

The December 1767 account combines cash receipts and payments, named counterparties or purposes, varied household/market/credit transactions and a balance. It belongs to a long-running ledger/account series rather than an isolated narrative document.

## RF191-W1 — what social and economic relationships are visible when the month's entries are treated as transactions rather than isolated lines?

### Question

> What kinds of household, kinship, labour, credit and market relationships are represented in the December 1767 cash transactions, and how do those relations connect to Washington's broader household/plantation economy?

### Why this question emerged

The account repeatedly names people/purposes and transaction types: rent, weaving, loans, household expenses, food, payments and family transfers.

### Why scientifically interesting

The Financial Papers are explicitly valuable for reconstructing private/business life and the people surrounding Washington. Account-book scholarship likewise treats transactions as evidence of social/economic relations.

### Discriminating / advancing evidence

- account/index records for named counterparties;
- adjacent monthly/annual accounts;
- corresponding ledgers, receipts and business correspondence;
- external evidence about roles/status of named people where responsibly identifiable.

### Boundary

The month alone cannot establish durable relationship structure or representativeness. Transaction labels also require historical contextual interpretation.

### Disciplinary challenge

Library of Congress and DEPCHA directly support the use of financial records for social/economic relation research. DEPCHA also warns implicitly that goods/services/currency meanings are historically contextual rather than self-evident categories.

**Disposition:** strong serious question at transaction/network scale.

### Dependency provenance

Generated from the primary transaction list; source-type scholarship strengthened the move from individual lines to relational analysis.

---

## RF191-W2 — how representative is December 1767 of Washington's cash practice?

### Question

> Which features of December 1767 are routine, seasonal or exceptional when compared with adjacent months and the longer General Ledger A sequence?

### Why this question emerged

A single month contains a heterogeneous set of receipts/payments and a balance, while the source is explicitly serial.

### Why scientifically interesting

It prevents a convenient single-month snapshot from being mistaken for typical economic behaviour and can reveal seasonal or episodic patterns.

### Discriminating / advancing evidence

- adjacent 1767–1768 cash accounts;
- annual patterns in General Ledger A;
- known travel, plantation or household events that can explain anomalies;
- consistent treatment of currency/account categories across periods.

### Boundary

Comparison requires attention to changes in recording practice. Founders Online notes for 1761 that the recorded date can sometimes be the date of noting rather than the transaction itself and that account organization changed in some years.

### Disciplinary challenge

Serial comparison is source-type appropriate, but the editorial warning means chronology must be source-critically reconstructed rather than treated as a ready-made time series.

**Disposition:** strong serious question with explicit source-critical control.

### Dependency provenance

Generated by recognizing the document as part of a serial ledger. Editorial notes about dating materially changed the proposed comparison method.

---

## RF191-W3 — how did cash, lending, household consumption and plantation/business activity interact?

### Question

> How were cash receipts/payments, personal or kin loans, household consumption and plantation/business transactions combined within Washington's financial practice in this period?

### Why this question emerged

December entries mix rent, weaving, cards, loans, expenses, oysters, family/pocket money, servants, food and payments to named people.

### Why scientifically interesting

It can test how categories modern researchers might separate were actually recorded and managed within one financial system.

### Discriminating / advancing evidence

- broader ledger accounts and linked receipts/invoices;
- Financial Papers indexing and project data;
- account-book expertise on debit/credit and category semantics;
- contextual evidence about household and plantation administration.

### Boundary

Modern categories such as `household`, `business` or `consumption` cannot simply be projected onto entries without historical justification. A structured-data model is one possible research instrument, not evidence for the categories themselves.

### Disciplinary challenge

DEPCHA supports semantic/relational analysis of account entries while its own modelling orientation also acts as a warning: the ontology/framework must not define the historical question in advance.

**Disposition:** serious but category-sensitive; requires explicit source criticism.

### Dependency provenance

Generated from heterogeneous transaction content; DEPCHA both supports and constrains the aggregation/semantic move.

## RF191-W4 — what does the account's recording practice allow us to infer about transaction chronology and bookkeeping procedure?

### Question

> How should the dates, `To`/`Contra` organization and balance in the cash accounts be interpreted as bookkeeping records, and when do they record the transaction itself versus the act of recording/settlement?

### Why this question emerged

The source has strong formal structure that can be mistaken for transparent event chronology.

### Why scientifically interesting

It establishes the source-critical basis on which later temporal/economic analysis depends.

### Discriminating / advancing evidence

- editorial notes across the Cash Accounts sequence;
- comparison with receipts, invoices and correspondence bearing independent dates;
- bookkeeping-history expertise;
- internal ledger cross-references.

### Boundary

A ledger date is not automatically an event timestamp. Founders Online explicitly warns for 1761 that Washington sometimes appears to assign the date when he noted a transaction.

### Disciplinary challenge

The challenge strongly supports this question because it directly tests the documentary mechanics of the evidence before aggregate historical inference.

**Disposition:** strong foundational source-critical question.

### Dependency provenance

Generated from document form; materially sharpened by the external editorial note about dates and recording practice.

# Part D — cross-fixture observation of question formation

## 6. Multiple serious questions were generated in all three fixtures

The experiment closes the narrowest form of the `absence-of-demonstration` gap.

Each fixture supported several distinct, non-trivial questions from the same current state without first selecting one unresolved micro-locus:

- Reg. Ii 5 produced institutional/economic, comparative-functional, qualitative/quantitative-boundary and transmission questions;
- Darwin Letter 5098 produced chronology, consequentiality, scientific-relation and material-provenance questions;
- Washington accounts produced relational, representativeness, category/financial-practice and bookkeeping/source-criticism questions.

This demonstrates that a rich state can support **plural serious next questions**.

It does not establish a generic algorithm for producing them.

## RF191-C1 — serious questions arose from relations and consequences more often than from isolated unresolved details

Across the three fixtures, the stronger questions were typically generated by asking about a relation already visible in the state:

- relations among several parish obligations/rules;
- relation between an uncertain date and correspondence chronology;
- relation between account entries and a serial/transactional system;
- relation between evidence precision and historical consequence.

Standalone residuals such as the Reg. Ii 5 `80` signs or the missing Darwin photograph did not become strong questions merely because they were unresolved.

### Research status

`cross-fixture recurrence in this bounded experiment`.

### Interpretation

A recurring operation in responsible question formation is **consequence-sensitive relation finding**, not backlog enumeration.

This is a descriptive Research finding, not a named system Capability.

## RF191-C2 — question formation repeatedly changed analytical scale only after the question made the enlargement relevant

Examples:

- Reg. Ii 5: from separate rule units to comparison across a cluster or wider visitation corpus;
- Darwin: from one letter to correspondence chronology/network or material provenance;
- Washington: from one entry/month to serial ledger and linked transaction evidence.

The enlargement was justified by the question, not by an intrinsic preference for macro analysis.

### Research status

`cross-fixture recurrence, consistent with RI186-03/RF185-C02`.

Question formation and analytical-scale choice are coupled. There is no evidence for a fixed source-unit→analysis-unit mapping before the question exists.

## RF191-C3 — responsible questions carried an evidential discriminator or a terminal evidence ceiling

The strongest generated questions could state what evidence would make a difference:

- additional visitation comparators/provenance;
- letter-series/material chronology clues;
- adjacent accounts/ledger/receipts.

They could also state when `no material consequence` or `insufficient evidence` would be a legitimate terminal outcome.

### Research status

`cross-fixture recurrence in generated-and-challenged questions`.

### Interpretation

A thin recurring scholarly operation is to connect a question to **discriminating evidence and a possible stopping condition**.

No score or fixed field set is required by this finding.

## RF191-C4 — source criticism changed or narrowed the question, not only the answer

The disciplinary challenge did not merely judge candidate answers. It changed question formulation:

- Reg. Ii 5 `how did the parish economy work?` became `what institutional/economic arrangement does the visitation record, and what additional evidence supports practice?`;
- Washington time-series questions acquired a warning that ledger dates can be recording dates rather than event dates;
- Darwin provenance/material questions became explicitly project-purpose-dependent;
- broad relationship questions were retained only where the fixture supplied an evidence anchor.

### Research status

`cross-fixture observation`.

### Interpretation

Source-critical expertise can be **constitutive of question formation itself**. It is not only a downstream validation layer.

This strengthens the earlier finding that transdisciplinary interaction can occur during inquiry rather than only after complete independent outputs.

---

## RF191-C5 — substantive question formation remains source-/purpose-sensitive despite recurring operations

The same generic prompt did not produce one homogeneous question class:

- a visitation source foregrounded institutional rights, recorded obligations, witness/transmission and the norm/practice distinction;
- a correspondence item foregrounded dating, relation chronology, scientific exchange and provenance;
- a ledger foregrounded seriality, aggregation, bookkeeping chronology and social/economic transactions.

Changing research purpose would also reprioritize questions within each fixture: e.g. Darwin editorial chronology versus history of scientific sociability; Washington documentary bookkeeping versus social/economic history.

### Research status

`cross-source recurrence of heterogeneity; strengthens RI186-09`.

### Interpretation

The experiment does **not** support a large homogeneous generic `Research Question Generation` operation that determines substantive questions independent of source/purpose.

What recurs is thinner: identify consequential relations, expose source-critical limits, identify discriminating evidence, allow scale/context to follow the question, and preserve multiple plausible directions until purpose/priority authority selects among them.

Whether later synthesis represents those recurrences as one Capability, several enabling functions or only constraints remains a post-Research decision.

---

## RF191-C6 — no evidence emerged for scoring, a universal Candidate Question object or a fixed competence lead table

The questions could be generated, challenged and compared qualitatively without:

- a numerical Value-of-Information score;
- a mandatory question schema;
- a permanent lead/contribute/review table;
- a fixed pre-execution stage sequence.

Different expertise became relevant because different questions exposed different evidence needs.

### Research status

`negative evidence within this bounded experiment`.

### Interpretation

The earlier Concept Candidate's specific representation/coordination forms remain more detailed than the positive question-generation evidence requires.

# Part E — targeted disciplinary-challenge result

## 7. What survived challenge

The literature-grounded challenge supports the credibility of several question families:

### Visitation source

- recorded rights/property/income and institutional arrangements;
- comparison of local obligations where source structure/provenance supports it;
- source-critical distinction between recorded norm/control situation and lived practice.

### Correspondence

- chronology/date inference using relational/material/provenance evidence;
- relation/correspondence questions where a broader corpus is explicitly required;
- preservation/provenance questions only when the research purpose makes them relevant.

### Accounts

- transactional/social/economic relations across entries;
- serial/aggregate comparison;
- bookkeeping/source-critical interpretation of dates, sides, balances and categories;
- historically contextual currency/goods/services interpretation.

## 8. What the challenge weakened or rejected

- unresolved Reg. Ii 5 `80` signs as a standalone priority;
- inferring actual parish practice directly from visitational prescriptions/records;
- a broad `resource-maintenance system` inferred from adjacency without wider recurrence;
- identifying the missing Darwin photograph from the thank-you note alone;
- assuming uncertain Darwin dating is automatically historically consequential;
- treating one month of Washington accounts as representative;
- treating ledger dates as transparent event timestamps;
